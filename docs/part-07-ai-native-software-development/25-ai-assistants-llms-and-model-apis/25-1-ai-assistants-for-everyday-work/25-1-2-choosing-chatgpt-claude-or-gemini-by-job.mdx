People ask "which AI is best?" the way they once asked "which text editor is best?" — as if the tool were the craft. The honest answer as of **November 2026**: all three flagships are excellent; they differ in shape, not in kind. The right choice depends on your job, your constraints, and how much you value a specific workflow. This lesson gives you a defensible way to pick — with real plan names and prices, verified the week this was written.

## 1. The Three Contenders at a Glance (Verified November 2026)

**ChatGPT (OpenAI)** — free tier; Go (~$8/mo); Plus ($20/mo); Pro at $100 / $200 / $500 per month tiers (GPT-6 Astra reasoning, longest sessions). Deep research, projects, memory, image creation. Strongest all-rounder and most integrations. API: GPT-6 family (flagship `gpt-6-astra`; `gpt-6-sol`, `gpt-6-terra`, `gpt-6-luna`) and the earlier GPT-5.x line. Source: [chatgpt.com/pricing](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/).

**Claude (Anthropic)** — free tier; Pro $20/mo (or $17/mo billed annually); Max $100/mo; Ultra $200/mo (renamed from Max 20x on 30 Oct 2026). Strong at long-document work, careful writing and structured analysis; Claude Code is included in Pro and above. API: Claude Fable 5.2, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5. Source: [claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing).

**Gemini (Google)** — free tier; Google AI Plus $4.99/mo; AI Pro $19.99/mo (Gemini 4 Argon access, 5 TB storage, Gemini in Workspace apps); AI Ultra $99.99/mo (5x limits) or $199.99/mo (20x). Deep Google Workspace integration (Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Drive); 1M-token context on Pro-class models. API: Gemini 4 Carbon / Argon / Hydrogen (Hydrogen free-tier available), Gemini 3.8 Flash, 3.1 Pro. Source: [gemini.google/subscriptions](https://gemini.google/subscriptions/).

Others worth knowing, briefly: **Perplexity** ($20/mo Pro, annual ~$17) — cited-source answer engine, excellent for research with the caveats in 25.1.3; **GitHub Copilot** (free tier; Pro $10/mo) — AI in the editor/IDE, Swimlane B; **Grok** (xAI; merged into "The X Subscription" in 2026, Premium ~$30/mo) — real-time X data; **DeepSeek** — free web chat, very low API prices, but a China-based provider (see the data-residency note below); **Mistral** — Le Chat renamed **Vibe** (May 2026, Pro $14.99/mo), the main European option and the easiest EU data-residency story.

**Prices drift.** Everything above is a snapshot from official pages on ~17 Nov 2026. Re-check before you pay.

## 2. Picking by Job Class

| Job | Usual best pick | Why — and the honest caveat |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Writer / content** | Claude (prose quality, voice consistency) or ChatGPT (fast, many formats) | Claude tends to hold a house style longer; ChatGPT's projects + memory are strong too. Caveat: all three AI-write recognizably when unedited — your edit is the differentiator. |
| **Researcher / analyst** | ChatGPT (deep research + web) or Perplexity (citations); Gemini if sources are Google Workspace docs | Deep research produces long cited reports; Perplexity surfaces links inline. Caveat: citations ≠ verification; see 25.1.5. |
| **Developer** | Claude (Sonnet 5.5 class, via Claude Code) or ChatGPT (GPT-6/Codex); Cursor if you want an agentic IDE | Coding strength is closer than marketing suggests; your *harness* (repo access, tests, editor flow) matters more than the model. Copilot if you live inside GitHub. |
| **Data / spreadsheet analyst** | ChatGPT or Gemini (Sheets/Excel integration); Claude for the analysis narrative | Both can read uploaded sheets and propose formulas. Gemini inside Sheets is the least-friction path if you're in Workspace. Caveat: never trust a formula you haven't checked on sample rows. |
| **Designer** | Gemini or ChatGPT for moodboards/Nano Banana–class image gen; Claude for design rationale and critique | Image-generation quality is a race that resets quarterly; check current examples, not 2024 reviews. |
| **Product manager / founder** | Claude for specs and PRDs (long-context); ChatGPT for broad surface area; both fine for research | Long documents (spec + meeting notes + feedback) favor Claude's 1M-token context. Founders: use it as a sparring partner, never as the decider. |
| **Admin / ops / EM** | Gemini if the company runs Workspace; Copilot if it runs Microsoft 365; ChatGPT as neutral default | The integration with your actual inbox/drive beats model subtleties. |
| **EU / regulated entity** | Mistral Vibe or Claude/Gemini/ChatGPT **Enterprise** with EU data processing terms | Data residency beats benchmark wins. Confirm the DPA, not the ad. |

## 3. The Same Subscription, Three Very Different Jobs — a Second Worked Example

*Ravi is a data analyst at a bank. Same employer, three use cases.*

1. **Recurring: auto-generating the weekly KPI narrative.** This is a templated, mid-capability task — a mid-tier model on the API with a fixed prompt, or even the consumer app with a saved prompt, is plenty. Cost on API: pennies per week. Pick: whatever's cheapest and compliant.
2. **One-off: "explain this 400-line legacy SQL procedure with no comments."** High-context, single-shot, value in accuracy. Claude (1M context) or ChatGPT with the procedure pasted in; verify the explanation by checking two claims against the code. Pick: whoever won your §7 bake-off for code comprehension.
3. **Regulated: customer complaint analysis.** Names, account data — leaves the consumer tier immediately. Either anonymize aggressively (strip names/IDs, keep the narrative) or use the bank's sanctioned enterprise deployment of any of the three. Pick: compliance decides, not you.

The skill is noticing the three needs are different before defaulting to "my favorite app."

## 4. Usage-Limit Reality — What "Unlimited" Never Means

Every plan in §1 is metered — messages per 5-hour window, weekly caps, or compute budgets. Nobody publishes exact counters; they shift with model cost per message. Practical signals you're near a limit: answers get shorter, the model picker greys out flagship models, or you're routed to a smaller model silently. Mitigations: use flagship models only for hard steps; do extraction/iteration in a mid-tier model; move bulk work to the API (cheaper, predictable); on the consumer side, batch non-urgent work across the week. If you hit limits weekly, the right tier is one step up — not a second $20 subscription to the same vendor.

## 5. Regional Availability and Access Notes

- Consumer apps availability and pricing vary by country (Google AI Pro/Ultra explicitly 150+ countries; OpenAI and Anthropic price in USD and differ by region/tax).
- DeepSeek: API has off-peak/peak pricing (off-peak ~half of peak as of late 2026); web chat is free.
- Enterprise procurement: all three majors sell through resellers and cloud marketplaces (AWS/Azure/GCP) — that's how you get committed-use discounts and unified billing if your org buys that way.

## 6. Worked Example — Picking for an Operations Manager

*Mara runs operations for a 40-person logistics firm in Frankfurt. Company runs Google Workspace. She writes SOPs, answers vendor emails, builds KPI spreadsheets, and summarizes weekly all-hands recordings.*

Step 1: list constraints — Workspace shop, EU customers (GDPR), German-English bilingual output, no IT department to vet a new tool.
Step 2: integration beats raw quality here. Gemini inside Gmail/Docs/Sheets removes copy-paste friction, and Workspace terms keep customer data out of training by default (Google states Workspace content is not used to train models without permission — see [Generative AI in Google Workspace Privacy Hub](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/generative-ai/generative-ai-in-google-workspace-privacy-hub)).
Step 3: cost check — AI Pro at $19.99/mo includes Workspace integration and 5 TB storage (useful). ChatGPT Plus at $20/mo is equally viable for writing, but no native Workspace grounding.
Step 4: pilot, don't commit — one month on AI Pro, measure hours saved drafting SOPs and emails, then decide. Benchmark claims from launch blog posts are not pilot data.

The pattern: **constraint list → integration → pilot → decide.** Never the reverse (see "benchmarks ≠ your workload").

## 7. Benchmarks ≠ Your Workload (Read Before Believing Any Chart)

Vendor launch charts show cherry-picked leaderboards. What matters: (a) does it handle *your* documents (your PDF formats, your domain jargon), (b) does it obey *your* output constraints, (c) is it available and allowed in your environment. A 2-point leaderboard gap routinely inverts on real work. How to do a 20-minute bake-off:

1. Collect 5 real tasks you do weekly (e.g., "summarize this 8-page SOP", "draft this customer reply", "extract line items from this invoice PDF").
2. Run all five on ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini free tiers, identical prompts.
3. Score each output 1–5 on correctness, tone fit, and time-to-usable.
4. Pick the winner *for your work*. Write down why — future-you will forget.

## 8. Data Residency and Privacy Differences (Where Relevant)

- Consumer tiers (Free/Plus/Pro, etc.): content **may be used to train models** unless you opt out. ChatGPT: turn off "Improve the model for everyone" (Settings → Data controls). Claude: opt out in privacy settings (consumer chats may be used for training otherwise). Gemini free: content used to improve products; Workspace accounts: not without permission.
- Business/Enterprise tiers (ChatGPT Enterprise/Business, Claude Team/Enterprise, Gemini in Workspace, Gemini API paid tier): not used for training by default; DPAs available; some offer regional processing (e.g., OpenAI regional endpoints ~10% price uplift; Anthropic US-only inference 1.1x; Google Vertex/Workspace CDPA).
- **DeepSeek** is operated by a company based in China; free-tier content and API traffic are subject to Chinese jurisdiction. Do not route client data, source code, or anything under NDA through it without explicit organizational approval.
- **Mistral (Vibe/Le Chat)** processes data in the EU — the default answer for European sovereignty requirements, subject to your DPA review.
- Full privacy protocol, including what never to paste anywhere, is in **25.1.5**.

## 9. Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| "The free tier got worse this month" | Silent model/limit changes — free tiers get the smallest models | Check the model picker; upgrade or switch provider for that task |
| "ChatGPT and Claude give opposite answers" | Both are probabilistic; prompts differ subtly | Normalize prompts; run 3 times each; check which is right |
| "Gemini can't see my file" | File type/size limits, or not on a plan with upload | Check plan limits; convert to PDF; remove scans |
| "My company banned AI tools" | Shadow-IT risk or unvetted data flow | Ask IT for the approved tier/enterprise agreement; don't improvise |
| "Benchmarks said X, I disagree" | Workload mismatch | Do the §7 bake-off; trust your five tasks |

## 9.5 Reading an AI Launch Announcement Without Getting Sold To

You will meet these weekly. The reliable tells of a marketing page: (1) a single benchmark chart with no task description; (2) "state-of-the-art" with no "on what, measured how"; (3) a demo GIF where the model answers perfectly on the first try; (4) no mention of refusals, latency, or cost. The reliable tells of substance: (1) named evals you can reproduce; (2) a model/system card with known limitations; (3) pricing and limits stated in the same paragraph as capability; (4) API model IDs you can call today. When both vendors launch within a week of each other, the honest move is §7's bake-off on your workload within 48 hours — leaderboard whiplash is a feature of this market, and your five tasks are the only stable metric you own.

## 9.6 The Five Questions to Ask Before Buying Any AI Seat

1. What are my top three tasks, and which tier's output passes my review on them this week?
2. Does my team/cluster/client data enter this account, and is it training-excluded here? (§8)
3. What is the exit — can I export history, and what happens to it on cancel?
4. Who in my org is the admin, and where are the retention and feedback toggles?
5. Is there a cheaper tier + 2 hours of prompting that does the same job? (Often yes; verify.)

## 9.7 A Decision Walk-Through — Product Launch Page

*Your team must ship a product landing page by Friday. Three assistants available. Who does what?*

1. **Competitive blurb research:** ChatGPT or Perplexity deep-research with citations (vendor docs, not blogs) → 30 minutes, verify three links.
2. **Hero copy + three headline options in brand voice:** Claude with your style guide pasted (it holds voice across a long doc); or ChatGPT with 2 examples. Either fine.
3. **React/Next.js implementation:** Cursor or Claude Code in the repo — it reads your component library and conventions; Swimlane B all the way.
4. **Final legal pass on claims:** *no model* — marketing, product and counsel sign off on real wording.
5. **Image variants:** Gemini/ChatGPT image generation, or your designer; either way, replace any invented stats with real ones.

Notice step 4: the professional stays accountable; AI does volume, humans do judgment. That ratio is the whole job description of the AI-native engineer in 2026.

## 10. Practice

**Guided exercise.** Do the §7 bake-off now: five real tasks, three assistants, a scored table. Thirty minutes, one decision.

**Independent exercise.** Write the one-paragraph justification for your $20 (or $0) spend next month, citing the tasks it will actually do.

**Assessment checklist**

- [ ] I can name current ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini plan prices (free / ~$20 / power tiers) and the trade they represent.
- [ ] I picked my assistant via constraints + pilot, not via a leaderboard.
- [ ] I know which consumer tiers train on my content and how to opt out.
- [ ] I know the EU-residency and China-jurisdiction answers for at least four providers.
- [ ] I can say "it depends — here's the 20-minute test" when asked which AI is best.
