Theory is cheap; workflows are rent. This lesson walks five workflows you will use this week. Each includes the **actual prompt**, a description of the output you will see, **two concrete improvements**, and **one failure mode with how to catch it**. The prompts are written to be pasted into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini as-is.

## (a) Weekly Status Email — the 5-minute Friday ritual

**Prompt (adapt names/metrics honestly):**

```text
You are my chief of staff. Write my weekly status email to my VP.
Facts (do not invent anything beyond these):
- Shipped: invoice-PDF parser now handles multi-page tables (error rate down from 12% to 4% on the test set).
- Shipped: staging deploys moved to the new runner; average deploy time 14min → 6min.
- Slipping: SSO work — blocked on the vendor's SAML cert renewal; new ETA Nov 28.
- Asked: approval to pause the dashboard rewrite for 2 weeks.
Tone: direct, confident, no corporate filler. Structure: Done / Slipped / Need from you.
Under 220 words. Flag, clearly at the end, any place where my facts are ambiguous.
```

**Output (described).** A tight three-section email. A good model will keep your numbers verbatim, mark "testing data vs production" as ambiguous (it is — your 4% is on a test set), and ask one clarifying question instead of inventing one metric.

**Improvement 1 — close the ambiguity it flags.** Add: *"Metrics are from the CI test set, not production. Say so explicitly in the email."* Now the VP can't quote "error rate down to 4%" into a board deck.

**Improvement 2 — set the reply expectation.** Add: *"End with a single yes/no question I need answered to unblock Monday: the dashboard pause approval."* This converts a report into a decision.

**Failure + catch.** The model silently upgrades your tentative "error rate down to 4%" into "accuracy now 96%." Catch: after generation, run **claims inventory** — highlight every number and proper noun in the draft, and confirm each against your source notes before sending. If a number isn't in your facts, it doesn't leave.

## (b) Meeting — from agenda to notes to action items

**Before:** send this agenda one day ahead (generated by the assistant):

```text
Draft a 30-minute agenda for: rollout of the new returns-label flow.
Attendees: warehouse lead, support lead, me. Decisions needed:
carrier default, exception path for undeliverables, comms date.
Format: timed blocks, owner per block, one decision question per block.
```

**During/after:** paste your raw notes (or the meeting transcript from Meet/Zoom — do not paste personal audio you don't own):

```text
Here are my raw meeting notes (messy). Produce:
1) A 5-bullet "what we decided" list, 2) Action items as a table:
owner / action / due / dependency, 3) Open questions with a suggested owner,
4) A follow-up email to attendees, under 150 words. Mark anything in my
notes that was ambiguous rather than inventing it.
Notes: carrier default UPS for this week pilot; undeliverables → refund queue
taged support; launch Nov 24 if labels ok; Priya flagged label printer lag;
Sam said comms can go Nov 21 regardless; open q - who watches the queue weekend?
```

**Output (described).** Decisions: UPS pilot for this week, refund queue for undeliverables, launch date conditional on labels. Action table: Priya—investigate printer lag—Nov 19; Sam—comms draft—Nov 21; you—monitor queue SLA—this weekend (unowned in notes → model should flag, not assign).

**Improvement 1 — demand the "decisions" section be falsifiable.** Add: *"Each decision must reference the exact line of my notes it came from."* This kills silent invention.

**Improvement 2 — time-box the follow-up email.** Add: *"Send it as if it goes from me, first person, and include a 3-bullet recap the recipients can reply 'ok' to."*

**Failure + catch.** The assistant assigns the orphaned weekend-queue item to a specific person who never agreed to it. Catch: in the action table, every owner must appear by name **in your notes** — if an owner doesn't, the row needs `[UNASSIGNED]`, not a name. Scan for it before forwarding.

## (c) Research Report — competitor pricing with citation discipline

**Prompt:**

```text
Research current self-serve pricing for linear.app, jira, and shortcut.com,
as of this month. For each: free tier limits, first paid tier price and seats,
and per-seat scaling. Rules:
- Every claim needs a URL to the vendor's own pricing page (not a blog).
- If you cannot find an official page, say "UNVERIFIED" — never guess a number.
- Separate "all three offer X" from "only Y offers Z."
- End with a table and a "what we should do about it" recommendation of ≤80 words.
```

**Output (described).** A table with rows per vendor and a sources list. A competent run shows: Linear free (small teams, basic features), first paid tier per seat; Jira free ≤10 users; Shortcut free tier; each with a link. It will usually **not** know unreleased/2026 re-pricing unless the model has browsing enabled — with browsing off, numbers will be stale or confidently wrong.

**Improvement 1 — force primary sources.** Add: *"Reject any source that is not linear.app, atlassian.com, or shortcut.com domains for pricing claims."* Comparison blogs drift; vendor pages are canonical.

**Improvement 2 — snapshot and date.** Add: *"Date-stamp every price and note that pricing is volatile; the table is a snapshot, not a budget."*

**Failure + catch.** The model cites a real vendor page but misreports the number on it (transposed digit, or a plan that no longer exists). Catch: open **every** URL in the sources list and re-read the pricing row yourself. Three minutes. Skipping this step is how "competitive intelligence" ends up wrong in front of a board.

## (d) Translation — a short doc into German

**Prompt:**

```text
Translate the following 400-word customer-facing change notice from English
to German (de-DE, formal "Sie"). Keep layout, headings, bullet structure and
all product names verbatim. After the translation, list any sentence where
German idiom forced a choice, and note which alternative you discarded and why.
<text> The maintenance window moves from Sunday 02:00–04:00 UTC to
Saturday 23:00–01:00 UTC... </text>
```

**Output (described).** A structurally faithful German notice, plus a short "translator's notes" section (e.g., how it rendered "maintenance window," whether "Wartungsfenster" was used; how it handled the time notation).

**Improvement 1 — bilingual review mode.** Add: *"Output the translation in a two-column table: original sentence | German, so a native speaker can review line by line."*

**Improvement 2 — register check.** Add: *"Confirm formal register throughout; flag any Austrian/Swiss German contamination (e.g., accidental 'ss' for ß or vice versa)."*

**Failure + catch.** Product names or regulatory wording get translated when they shouldn't be (or dates flip dd/MM vs MM/dd ambiguously). Catch: diff **all** numbers, times, dates and product names against the source with a simple script or by eye — they must be byte-identical except where the language genuinely requires a change. If a number moved, stop and check.

## (e) Executive Summary — 30 pages in, 1 page out

**Prompt (after uploading the PDF — see 25.1.4 for upload paths):**

```text
This PDF is our Q3 security audit (30 pages). Write a one-page executive
summary for the board, non-technical. Structure:
- Overall risk posture in one sentence.
- The 3 findings that matter, each: what it is / business impact / fix / owner / date.
- What is fine (so the board doesn't panic about it).
Rules: no finding may appear that is not explicitly in the document — give the
page number for each. Do not soften severity language. If the document is
inconsistent, say so.
```

**Output (described).** A one-page brief: posture sentence, three findings with page refs (e.g., "p.14 — exposed staging credential; rotate within 7 days — owner: platform team"), a "lower-priority/OK" paragraph, and any noted internal inconsistency of the report itself.

**Improvement 1 — demand an audit trail.** Add: *"Include a final 'Verification' block listing the page numbers used for each finding, so I can spot-check in five minutes."*

**Improvement 2 — pre-mortem the board's questions.** Add: *"Append the three hardest questions a board member might ask, with the one-line answer, drawn only from the document."*

**Failure + catch.** **Hallucinated finding** — the model invents a plausible critical issue ("unpatched OpenSSL") that is nowhere in the document, page ref and all, because audits "usually" have one. Catch: open the three cited pages and confirm each finding exists with the stated owner and date. Then skim the document's own findings table and confirm no *real* finding was dropped. This two-way check is non-negotiable for board material.

## 2. The Common Shape of All Five Workflows

Notice the pattern every time: **(1) role + (2) facts-only context + (3) structure of the output + (4) explicit "don't invent — flag instead" + (5) a verification hook you perform yourself.** Skipping step 5 is the difference between a power user and a gambler.

## 3. Enhancing Any of These Prompts

- Add your own **done-criteria**: "Done when: under 200 words, no invented figures, German B2 register."
- Add **negative constraints**: "Do not use the words 'leverage', 'synergy', 'seamless'."
- Ask for **alternatives, not answers**: "Give me two versions — one terse, one warm."
- Close the loop: paste the model's output back and ask *"Critique this as my most skeptical VP would. What would they push back on?"* — then edit.

## 4. Practice

**Guided exercise.** Run workflow (a) on your real notes from last Friday; verify every number against your actual tracking system.

**Independent exercise.** Run workflow (c) on your two closest competitors; open every citation; record which link was stale or wrong.

**Challenge.** Chain (b) → (e): raw notes → action items → a one-page "what the board should know about this project" summary. Verify the chain end to end.

**Assessment checklist**

- [ ] I have a working weekly-status prompt tuned to my VP's taste.
- [ ] My action-item outputs always mark unassigned owners, never invent them.
- [ ] I open every cited URL on research output before using it.
- [ ] My translations preserve numbers/dates/names exactly.
- [ ] I can explain the two-way verification check from workflow (e) without notes.
