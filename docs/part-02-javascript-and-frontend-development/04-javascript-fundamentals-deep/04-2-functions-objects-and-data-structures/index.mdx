Modern frontend and backend JavaScript spends most of its time transforming collections of records, configuring callbacks, and updating state safely without corrupting shared objects. Subchapter **04.2** teaches functions as values, how JavaScript objects and prototypes actually resolve properties, why mutating nested references creates silent bugs, and how to combine `Array` methods, `Map`, `Set`, and structural copying cleanly.

## Lessons in Subchapter 04.2

1. **[04.2.1 First-Class Functions and Higher-Order Patterns](/book/part-02-javascript-and-frontend-development/04-javascript-fundamentals-deep/04-2-functions-objects-and-data-structures/04-2-1-first-class-functions-and-higher-order-patterns):** Declarations vs. expressions vs. arrows, callbacks, currying, composition, and `this` differences.
2. **[04.2.2 Objects, Prototypes and References](/book/part-02-javascript-and-frontend-development/04-javascript-fundamentals-deep/04-2-functions-objects-and-data-structures/04-2-2-objects-prototypes-and-references):** Stack vs. heap references, shallow vs. deep cloning (`structuredClone`), prototype chains, and `class` syntax under the hood.
3. **[04.2.3 Arrays, Maps, Sets and Immutability](/book/part-02-javascript-and-frontend-development/04-javascript-fundamentals-deep/04-2-functions-objects-and-data-structures/04-2-3-arrays-maps-sets-and-immutability):** `map`/`filter`/`reduce`, non-mutating array updates (`toSorted`, spread), `Map` vs. plain objects, `Set` deduplication, and destructuring/rest/spread.
