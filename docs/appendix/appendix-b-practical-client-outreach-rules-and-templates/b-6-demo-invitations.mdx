**What this solves:** They said yes to a call — now you need them to show up, engage, and remember you. Most demo invites are "here's my calendar link" — lazy and forgettable.

## The Demo Invitation Structure

```text
Subject: [Their company] — the 15-minute walkthrough you asked for

Hi [Name],

Confirmed for [date/time]. Here's the agenda (15 minutes):

 1. Your checkout 401 story — what I saw, what it means (5 min)
 2. The fix — architecture in 90 seconds, then live code (7 min)
 3. What it looks like for your data — I'll use a test store so you see
    it end-to-end (2 min)
 4. Next steps (1 min)

Bring: your webhook logs or a laptop with Shopify admin open — or not,
I'll drive.

See you [Day], [Time].
[Your name]
```

## Why This Works

- **Agenda upfront** — they know it'll be short, focused, valuable
- **Evidence you did the homework** — shows you did real work before the call
- **Low barrier** — no preparation required, just presence
- **Specific outcome** — they know exactly what they'll walk away with

## Micro-Demos (Instead of Full Calls)

```text
"Alice — instead of a full demo, want me to just send you a 2-minute
Loom of the fix running against a test store? You can scrub through it
when you have 2 minutes.
```

**Loom demo link structure:**
- 0:00 — Their pain, restated in one sentence
- 0:20 — The fix, one architecture slide
- 0:45 — Live code, one test passing
- 1:30 — The metric they'll see

**Why Loom > calendar:** no scheduling friction; they watch on their time; you get replies instead of no-shows.

## Follow-Up After the Demo

```text
"Ben — enjoyed walking through it today. Quick recap:
 — You'll see 401s drop inside a week
 — Full fix: 2 weeks, $18k
 — I'll send the one-page proposal tomorrow

Anything I should adjust before then?"
```
