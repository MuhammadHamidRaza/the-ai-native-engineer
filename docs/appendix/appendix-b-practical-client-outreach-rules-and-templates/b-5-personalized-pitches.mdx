**What this solves:** You know the pain is real; now you need to frame your offer as the fix. The personalized pitch is where most deals die — too generic, too long, too much about you.

## The 3-Sentence Pitch That Works

```text
[1 observation about their pain, with a number]
[1 sentence — what you'd do, in outcome terms]
[1 question — "Want me to sketch how?"]
```

**Example:**

```text
"Alice — you said the webhook failures cost ~$12k/mo in refunds and
support time. I'd put a signed-payload idempotency layer in place that
kills the silent failures; two weeks, risk-free, you keep full control.
Want me to sketch the architecture?"
```

**Why this works:** demonstrates you listened; offers risk-free path; asks a question they can say yes to in one word.

## The Pitch Formula by Buyer Type

| Buyer | Lead with | One-sentence close |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Technical founder | Architecture detail | "Want the 2-page spec?" |
| Non-technical founder | Revenue/cost/risk | "Want a 15-min walkthrough?" |
| Ops manager | Time saved, headcount | "Want me to pilot it on one store?" |
| CTO/Head of Eng | Reliability/scale | "Want the capacity plan?" |

## Pitch Framing Rules

1. Their pain, first — never your capabilities
2. Outcome, not method — they don't care about your stack yet
3. One metric they care about — revenue, cost, time, risk
4. Low-friction next step — spec, walkthrough, pilot
5. No attachments — the pitch lives in the message

## Pitch vs Demo

- **Pitch** — gets the meeting scheduled
- **Demo** — gets the deal signed (Appendix B.6 and A.8)

Never pitch the demo; always pitch the next step.

## The No-Permission Pitch (for warm relationships)

```text
"Ben — I know you don't need me to fix checkout; you've got it handled.
But if you want a second pair of eyes on the 401 spike, happy to look
for 30 minutes. No charge, no pitch — just a favor between engineers."
```

**Why it works:** gives an out; asks for nothing; positions as peer. Paradoxically, it opens the door wider than a polished pitch.
