**What this solves:** You suspect a pain; they haven't confirmed it. Most novices pitch into an unconfirmed wound — and get ignored. This section gives you the exact phrasing to confirm pain without being pushy.

## The Confirmation Questions

**For revenue leak:**

```text
"You mentioned webhook failures cost you X customers/month — have you
modeled what that's worth annually, or is it still a feeling?"
```

**For speed drag:**

```text
"You said deploys take 40 minutes — is that blocking production fixes,
or just annoying?"
```

**For cost excess:**

```text
"Your cloud bill grew 60% YoY — is that a budget conversation or a
burning issue?"
```

**For risk exposure:**

```text
"You flagged compliance gaps in your last audit — is that on the board's
radar, or just yours?"
```

## The Funnel That Works

```text
Observation → Question → Silence
```

**Do not fill the silence.** Buyers who talk into the pause will tell you the truth — often more than you expected. The moment you rush to fill it, you lose the truth and become another vendor.

## Confirmation Signals (Verbal + Tonal)

| Signal | What it means |
| :-- | :-- |
| "Huh, that's a good question" | You hit the nerve |
| "Actually, it's worse than that…" | Confirmed pain |
| Silence >3 seconds | They're doing the math — let them |
| "Let me check with my team" | Real issue, real process |
| "We're fine" | Different pain — ask two more questions |

## The Last-Question Gate

Before proposing anything, ask:

```text
"If you could fix this one thing in the next 90 days, what would change
for the business?"
```

**Why:** if they answer with a specific number or outcome, you have a real opportunity; if they shrug, there isn't one — politely exit.
