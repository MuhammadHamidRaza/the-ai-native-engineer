**Purpose:** Sales skills are physical skills — they require reps under pressure, not reading about objections. This section gives you structured role-play scenarios that mirror real client conversations.

## How to Run These Exercises

1. Find a partner (another engineer, a friend, or a coworker) — or record yourself solo
2. Assign roles: one plays you, one plays the buyer
3. Run the scenario, 10–15 minutes
4. Debrief: what worked, what froze, what surprised you
5. Swap roles and repeat
6. Do all 10 scenarios at least once — some twice

## The 10 Scenarios

**Scenario 1 — The Budget Objection**
Buyer: "We don't have $20k for this right now."
Your job: reframe to cost of inaction, offer pilot, don't discount.

**Scenario 2 — The Competitor Mention**
Buyer: "Vendor X quoted us $8k for the same thing."
Your job: differentiate on outcome, not price; don't attack them.

**Scenario 3 — The "We'll Think About It"**
Buyer: "Let us discuss internally and get back to you."
Your job: ask what specifically they need to see; offer to answer it now.

**Scenario 4 — The Scope Creep Test**
Buyer mid-demo: "Oh, can you also handle our email flows while you're here?"
Your job: "Happy to — that's a separate scope; want me to add it to the proposal?"

**Scenario 5 — The Technical Interrogation**
Buyer (CTO): "Walk me through your idempotency strategy — why that order of operations?"
Your job: answer deeply, confidently, without jargon walls.

**Scenario 6 — The Silent Decision-Maker**
Buyer: [VP silent through the whole call]
Your job: address them directly with one respectful question.

**Scenario 7 — The "Free Work" Ask**
Buyer: "Can you just fix this one thing for free so we can see if you're any good?"
Your job: decline gracefully, offer paid audit or small pilot.

**Scenario 8 — The Urgent Crisis**
Buyer: "Our checkout is down RIGHT NOW. Can you fix it today?"
Your job: triage, set expectation, offer emergency rate if applicable.

**Scenario 9 — The Wrong Buyer**
Buyer: "I don't have the budget authority, but I love it."
Your job: ask who does, offer to join the budget conversation.

**Scenario 10 — The "No" That Isn't Final**
Buyer: "Not this quarter. Maybe Q1."
Your job: lock in a Q1 check-in date before hanging up.

## Debrief Questions (After Every Role-Play)

1. What was the turning point — who led the frame shift?
2. Did you listen more than you spoke?
3. Did you quantify the pain before proposing anything?
4. Did you get a concrete next step, not a vague "we'll talk"?
5. What will you do differently next time?

## Scoring (Peer-Rated)

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: |
| Listening ratio | Dominant speaker | Balanced | Mostly listening |
| Pain quantification | Skipped | Hinted | Asked and confirmed |
| Next step clarity | Vague | Somewhat clear | Dated, specific |
| Composure under pressure | Frozen | Recovered | Calm, redirected |

**Target: ≥16/20 across 5 scenarios before live outreach.**
