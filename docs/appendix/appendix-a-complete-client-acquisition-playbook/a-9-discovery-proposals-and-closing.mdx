**What this solves:** You've qualified the lead and they want to talk. (Reusable proposal templates live in **B.8 Proposal Templates**; the worked examples here show the narrative around them.) Discovery, proposals, and closing are a single motion — most clients decide during discovery, not after the proposal. This playbook runs them as one flow.

## The Discovery Call Structure (45 Minutes)

```text
0:00–0:05 — Warm open, agenda, permission to ask hard questions
0:05–0:15 — Their world: team, stack, current process (80% listening)
0:15–0:25 — The pain: frequency, cost, who feels it, what's been tried
0:25–0:30 — The dream: what success looks like in 90 days (their words)
0:30–0:40 — Fit check: scope, timeline, budget range (ask directly)
0:40–0:45 — Next steps: proposal by Friday, decision by next Friday
```

**Questions that unlock budget:**
- "If this stayed broken for another year, what does that cost?"
- "Who owns this number today — and what happens to them if nothing changes?"
- "Have you budgeted for this, or is this exploratory?"

## The Proposal That Closes

Deliver within 48 hours. One page. Structure:

```text
Client: ______
Problem (their words, quoted): ______
Outcome (metric + timeframe): ______
Scope of work:
  Week 1 — Discovery + audit
  Week 2–3 — Implementation + testing
  Week 4 — Deploy + train your team
Investment: $X
  50% upfront, 50% on delivery (or Net 14 after go-live)
Out of scope: [list 2–3 things explicitly to prevent scope creep]
Timeline: start [date], live [date]
```

**Attach nothing.** The proposal is the document — decks and PDFs signal uncertainty.

## Closing Without Pressure

```text
"I sent the proposal Friday. What questions do you have?"
```

Then **silence**. Let them fill it. Most clients close in the silence. If they say "we need to think about it", ask: "What would you need to see to move forward?" — and answer exactly that.

## The Two Killer Follow-Ups

1. **"Here's what I'd do in week 1"** — a 5-line concrete plan showing you understood them
2. **"I have two other clients interested in this slot — can I hold it until Friday?"** — honest scarcity

## Common Failure Modes

- **Talking too much in discovery** — you learn nothing; they feel unheard
- **Proposing before pain is quantified** — price is always "too high"
- **"Let me think about it" with no follow-up date** — chase or lose
- **Discounting under pressure** — teach them you're not desperate
