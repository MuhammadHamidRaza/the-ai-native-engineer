**What this solves:** Marketplaces look crowded and hostile. For technical AI/full-stack work they're still the fastest path to a first paid project — if you select projects, bid strategically, and protect your rate.

## Choosing the Right Marketplace

| Platform | Best for | Watch out for |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Upwork** | Serious clients, $5k–$50k projects | Proposal fatigue — must differentiate |
| **Toptal** | Elite rates, vetted network | ~3% acceptance (Toptal-reported; verify current) |
| **Contra** | Direct relationships, portfolio | Smaller pipeline, fees on some plans |
| **Freelance.com** | Breadth, quick wins | Price competition |
| **Fiverr** | Productized gigs | Race-to-bottom pricing |

## Upwork Playbook (The Realistic One)

1. **Title** = niche + outcome: "AI Engineer — I Build Support Deflection Systems for Shopify"
2. **Profile overview** = 3 sentences: who you help, what outcome, proof (one metric)
3. **Skills** = exact stack the client searches
4. **Portfolio** = 3 case studies with before/after numbers
5. **Proposals** = 100–150 words; reference their job post specifically; never copy-paste

## Bid Discipline

- Bid only jobs posted &lt;24h ago with real budget ($2k+)
- 10 tailored proposals/week > 50 generic ones
- Track win rate; under 15% means your positioning is off
- Charge $75–125/hr minimum; if the client wants bargains, walk

## Graduate Off Marketplaces

After 2–3 wins: ask for testimonials, build direct relationship, move next project off-platform (respect ToS), offer retainer. Marketplaces are a launchpad, not a home.

## Red Flags

- "We'll hire you for 1 hour, then decide on more work" — always a bait
- Asking to move to WhatsApp/email before payment protection
- Vague scope, no budget, no deadline — never bid
- Asking for free work "as a test" — never do it
