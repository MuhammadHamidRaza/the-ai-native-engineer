Subchapter **11.1** turns fuzzy product requirements into crisp **Entity-Relationship (`ER`) models**, enforceable keys, and balanced schemas that prevent duplicate or orphaned data.

## Lessons in Subchapter 11.1

1. **[11.1.1 Entities, Keys and Cardinality](/book/part-04-databases-backend-and-full-stack-development/11-database-design-and-fundamentals/11-1-data-modeling-and-relationships/11-1-1-entities-keys-and-cardinality):** Designing the **ShopCore** schema (`users`, `categories`, `products`, `orders`, `order_items`), PKs vs. FKs, and 1:1 / 1:N / M:N relationships.
2. **[11.1.2 Normalization and Denormalization](/book/part-04-databases-backend-and-full-stack-development/11-database-design-and-fundamentals/11-1-data-modeling-and-relationships/11-1-2-normalization-and-denormalization):** Fixing repeating groups and update anomalies (`1NF`, `2NF`, `3NF`) plus knowing when intentional denormalization (historical snapshots) is mandatory.
3. **[11.1.3 The ShopCore Schema](/book/part-04-databases-backend-and-full-stack-development/11-database-design-and-fundamentals/11-1-data-modeling-and-relationships/11-1-3-the-shopcore-schema):** The canonical DDL and seed data (users, categories, products, orders, order_items, payments) that every later lesson runs against — your single source of truth for this book.
