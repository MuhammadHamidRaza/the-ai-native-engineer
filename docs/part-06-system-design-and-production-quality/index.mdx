Parts 01–05 got ShopCore built, tested, deployed, and monitored. **Part 06 is where it becomes a system you can defend** — under traffic, under failure, under the audit of a stranger who shows up at 3 a.m. and has to keep it running. Production quality is not a checklist you apply at the end; it is a set of engineering muscles: thinking in systems, attacking and testing your own work, observing reality instead of guessing, and making the database fast enough that it never has to be the excuse.

## The Four Chapters in This Part

| Ch | Title | Focus hours | The question it answers |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 21 | Software Architecture & System Design | 18 | What shape should this system have — and why this shape, not another? |
| 22 | Security, Testing & Observability | 12 | How do I know the system works, and how will I know when it breaks? |
| 23 | Performance & Scalability | 8 | How fast is it, what breaks first, and what do I add next? |
| 24 | Database Performance & Operations | 12 | Why is the database slow, and how do I operate it at scale? |

**Hours honesty:** 50 focused hours total for Part 06. Chapters 21–22 build the judgment and the proof; 23–24 are where you learn to find and fix the physical bottlenecks that cap everything you built before.

Each chapter follows the same arc: **mental model → measurement → fix → verify**. No chapter in this part is "read and nod." Every one ends in something you ran, measured, and wrote down.

## What You Will Be Able to Do by the End of Part 06

Concretely, on the real ShopCore codebase, you will be able to:

- Draw the ShopCore bounded-context map from memory, defend the monolith-vs-services decision in an ADR, and explain the consistency model your checkout actually offers.
- Apply the OWASP Top 10:2025 to ShopCore's real routes, rotate a leaked Stripe key in under 30 minutes, and make CI fail on a broken contract test.
- Compute checkout's error budget from real metrics, page on symptoms (not causes), and write a blameless postmortem your team can act on.
- Read a Lighthouse report and CrUX data for the ShopCore storefront, enforce a JavaScript bundle budget in CI, and prove the fix with before/after numbers.
- Profile a Node.js checkout process with `node --prof`, load-test it with k6 at 100 VUs, and state its p50/p95/p99 with thresholds that fail the build.
- Put a cache-aside layer in front of the catalog with single-flight stampede protection and invalidation-on-write, plus a Redis-backed rate limiter on `POST /v1/orders`.
- Read an `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` plan, build the composite/covering/partial/GIN index the query needs, and prove it with `pg_stat_user_indexes` and timing.
- Set up PgBouncer in transaction-pooling mode, and explain why `max_connections = 100` and an app pool of 10 per pod can silently exhaust every connection Postgres has.
- Roll a read replica, read its lag, and partition `orders` by month — with a written migration plan that does not lose a row.

## How to Work Through This Part

1. Do Chapter 21 before 22–24. Architecture decisions made after measurement are informed guesses; decisions made before you even know the shape are superstition.
2. Do Chapter 22's load-adjacent work (22.3 observability) before Chapter 23 — performance work without metrics is driving at night with the headlights off.
3. Do Chapter 23 before Chapter 24. Half of "database performance" is really "we scaled the app tier into a wall the database couldn't hold."
4. Keep a running `shopcore-perf-notebook.md` (outside this repo, or in your own scratch space): every number you measure, the command that produced it, and what changed after the fix. That notebook is your evidence at the exit gate.

**Prerequisites:** Part 04 (ShopCore built and deployable; see [15.4 Verification & Shipping](/book/part-04-databases-backend-and-full-stack-development/15-complete-full-stack-application/15-4-verification-and-shipping)) and Part 05 (deployed ShopCore; see [20.2 Production Reliability](/book/part-05-deployment-cloud-and-devops/20-kubernetes-production-scaling-and-multi-cloud/20-2-production-reliability-scaling-and-observability)). If you have never seen an EXPLAIN plan or a Grafana panel, budget the top of every range.

## Part 06 Exit Gate

You may consider Part 06 complete when, on the real ShopCore deployment, you can:

- Produce a **performance report** with Lighthouse + k6 + `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` before/after numbers you measured yourself, and point to the exact line that was slow.
- Survive the **3 a.m. drill**: given a checkout-latency page, walk from symptom (SLO burn) → RED metrics → trace → DB plan → fix, in under 30 minutes, out loud.
- Show a **connection budget** (`pg_stat_activity`, pool sizes, PgBouncer config) that cannot exceed `max_connections`, and explain exactly what breaks when it can.
- Present the **capacity arithmetic** for ShopCore: what peak traffic it holds today, what fails first, and the cheapest next step (cache, index, replica, or a bigger instance).
- Hand a teammate the **runbook** for "orders table is slow" and watch them execute it without you.

If any of those five require you to go look something up mid-drill, the gate is not passed yet — loop back to the chapter that covers it.
