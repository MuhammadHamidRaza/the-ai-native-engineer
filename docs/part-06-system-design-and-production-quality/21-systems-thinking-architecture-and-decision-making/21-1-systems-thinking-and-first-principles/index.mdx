**Subchapter 21.1** is the foundation the rest of Part 06 stands on. Before you compare monoliths to services or draw a context map, you need the three thinking moves every senior engineer actually uses: see the system, pick the right move, and carry a toolkit of reasoning patterns.

## Lessons in Subchapter 21.1

1. **[21.1.1 What a System Is: Components, Boundaries, Feedback and Emergence](/book/part-06-system-design-and-production-quality/21-systems-thinking-architecture-and-decision-making/21-1-systems-thinking-and-first-principles/21-1-1-what-a-system-is-components-boundaries-feedback-emergence):** Components, boundaries and environment; inputs → outputs; negative and positive feedback loops; delays; emergence; failure propagation (ShopCore's stock-emerges-after-webhook case); complexity and Cynefin; causation vs correlation; three modeling exercises on ShopCore.
2. **[21.1.2 System Thinking vs System Design: The Two Moves](/book/part-06-system-design-and-production-quality/21-systems-thinking-architecture-and-decision-making/21-1-systems-thinking-and-first-principles/21-1-2-system-thinking-vs-system-design-the-two-moves):** The mandatory comparison — when to map relationships vs when to commit to components; side-by-side worked Thinking and Design diagrams of ShopCore; the three scenarios where teams fail either move; solving fraud rollouts, holiday surges and partial outages with both moves in sequence.
3. **[21.1.3 The Thinking Toolkit: First Principles, Decomposition, Abstraction and Modeling](/book/part-06-system-design-and-production-quality/21-systems-thinking-architecture-and-decision-making/21-1-systems-thinking-and-first-principles/21-1-3-thinking-toolkit-first-principles-decomposition-abstraction-modeling):** First principles (rebuild the price engine), in-place decomposition (checkout: capture / verify / settle), abstraction (payment surrogate), context/domain/deployment diagrams and state machines, failure trees, probabilistic reasoning (p99, SLO math) — each with a ShopCore scenario and a checklist.

Work this subchapter in order. 21.1.1 is the vocabulary; 21.1.2 is the discipline; 21.1.3 is the toolbox. If you skim 21.1.2, every later design decision in this book will feel arbitrary.

**Exit checklist**

- [ ] I can draw the ShopCore context diagram and label the payment webhook boundary.
- [ ] I can name the one missing feedback loop in ShopCore's stock flow and what happens without it.
- [ ] I can state, for a given change, which move I am in — Thinking or Design — and why.
- [ ] I can rebuild ShopCore's pricing rule from first principles on a whiteboard.
