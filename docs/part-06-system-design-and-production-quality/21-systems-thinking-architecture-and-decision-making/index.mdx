Every architectural failure is a thinking failure first: the team designed components before understanding the system, or understood the system and never translated it into a design. **Chapter 21 (`18 Focused Hours`)** closes that gap — from the way a system actually behaves to the contracts and module boundaries that make it buildable.

## Chapter 21 Structure

- **21.1 Systems Thinking & First Principles:** What a system is (components, boundaries, feedback, emergence), the explicit comparison between System Thinking and System Design, and the thinking toolkit — first principles, decomposition, abstraction, modeling.
- **21.2 Requirements, Decisions & Domain Modeling:** Requirements engineering from discovery to acceptance criteria; ADRs, trade-offs and build vs buy; domain modeling and bounded contexts.
- **21.3 Architecture & Distributed Systems:** Modular monoliths vs distributed services, API architecture, sync vs event-driven communication.
- **21.4 Distributed Systems Resilience:** Consistency models and CAP, timeouts, retries, circuit breakers, queues and backpressure.

By the end of this chapter you will be able to take an ambiguous business problem — "our checkout breaks under load" — and (1) map the system producing the pain, (2) write acceptance criteria a team can build against, and (3) design the architecture with explicit trade-offs.
