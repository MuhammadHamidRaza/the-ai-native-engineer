**Subchapter 29.2** is the chapter your future incident reviewer will thank you for. An agent without hard boundaries is a process you cannot kill gracefully, an invoice you cannot predict, and an action you cannot explain. This subchapter defines the boundaries: when the agent must stop, who must approve, and where it lands when everything fails.

## Lessons in Subchapter 29.2

1. **[29.2.1 Stopping Criteria, Budgets and Human Approvals](/book/part-08-ai-applications-agents-mcp-and-automation/29-agent-architecture-and-agentic-ai/29-2-reliability-boundaries-for-agents/29-2-1-stopping-criteria-budgets-and-human-approvals):** The five stopping criteria, a worked budget derivation, tiered approval gates, dormant-agent risk, `max_turns` in the SDK plus a watchdog, and the Postmaster forbidden-actions checklist.
2. **[29.2.2 Failure Recovery and Deterministic Fallbacks](/book/part-08-ai-applications-agents-mcp-and-automation/29-agent-architecture-and-agentic-ai/29-2-reliability-boundaries-for-agents/29-2-2-failure-recovery-and-deterministic-fallbacks):** The failure taxonomy, the retry → narrow → escalate ladder, the always-available deterministic fallback, state snapshots, idempotency keys, and the postmortem template.

Work 29.2.1 before 29.2.2 — budgets and gates define what "failure" means for the recovery design.
