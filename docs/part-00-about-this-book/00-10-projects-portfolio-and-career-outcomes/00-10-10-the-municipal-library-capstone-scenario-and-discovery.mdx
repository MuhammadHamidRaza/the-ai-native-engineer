This is the **integrated capstone** — the one project that proves you can do everything Parts 01–08 teach, in the order a real client needs it. It uses the **ShopCore codebase you built in Parts 04–06** ([11.1.3 canonical schema](/book/part-04-databases-backend-and-full-stack-development/11-database-design-and-fundamentals/11-1-data-modeling-and-relationships/11-1-3-the-shopcore-schema), [15.x full-stack app](/book/part-04-databases-backend-and-full-stack-development/15-complete-full-stack-application), Part 05 deployment, Part 06 design) and extends it into a new domain for a new customer: a **public library system called Chapterhouse**.

Do this capstone after Part 08. It uses every layer: computers and HTTP (Part 01), typed frontend (02), Python where appropriate (03), databases/backend (04), deployment (05), system design (06), AI-native workflow (07), RAG assistant (08).

## 1. The Scenario — A Real Brief, Not a Tutorial

> **From:** Priya Nair, Director of Library Services, City of Kestrelville (fictional municipality)
>
> "Our library catalog is a 2009 Access database one volunteer maintains. Members can't see availability online; holds happen by phone. We want a public online catalog with account login, search, and hold requests, plus an assistant that can answer policy questions (loan periods, late fees, renewals, membership eligibility) from our official policy documents — and nothing else. We have a deadline (budget cycle ends March 31) and a small budget. Our patrons include seniors on basic phones, teenagers, and one person in the city who is a librarian by training — Karen — who has the only institutional knowledge and retires in June."

**What you are given:**

- `sample-catalog.csv` — 2,000 rows: `isbn, title, author, year, copies_total, copies_available, category` (fictional data, no real persons).
- `policies/` — five policy PDFs (see § 5 of `00.10.15` for the corpus): loans, renewals, late fees, membership, privacy.
- `legacy-export-notes.md` — notes describing the old Access database's quirks (inconsistent categories, `copies=0` meaning "unknown", year 0000 for "undated").
- Constraints: no PII may be sent to any external LLM; the assistant must cite policy text or refuse; the reservation flow must work **even when the AI is down**; hosting budget ~ $0–20/mo; launch with a soft release to one branch first.

**Deliverables:** a working Catalog + Holds application with an AI policy assistant, deployed and handed over, in nine stages. Stages map onto `00.10.10`–`00.10.14`.

## 2. Stage 1 — Discovery: Questions Before Code

Write your discovery questions **first**, before looking at any data. The brief rewards curiosity, not completionism. A strong set covers:

| Area | Questions |
| :-- | :-- |
| Users | Who are the three most important patron personas? How many active cardholders? Any accessibility requirements (screen readers, large text, print-first)? |
| Business objectives | Is the goal visits, circulation, program signups, or cost reduction? What metric moves if this works? |
| Current workflow | How do holds work today, exactly? Walk me through one phone hold. Where do they break? |
| Catalog data | Which fields are trustworthy? What does `copies=0` mean? What categories exist — how many distinct, any free-text? |
| Reservation rules | Hold expiry? Max active holds per member? Priority: first-come, or members vs non-residents? Can staff override? |
| Permissions | What can a member see/do? Staff roles? What can Karen do that you must not lose? |
| Privacy | What must never reach an LLM? Where is data stored? Retention after card expiry? |
| Operations | Who runs this after launch? Backups? What if the assistant is wrong? Incident process? |
| Performance | Peak concurrency (opening hours? after school?), expected search latency, budget *for real* (infra + your hours)? |
| Rollback | If launch goes badly, what is the rollback? Who decides? |

**Deliverable:** a one-page Discovery Brief (template in `00.10.11`) capturing the answers *in the client's words* — including the uncomfortable ones ("Karen retires in June" and "the data is dirty" are the two that matter most).

## 3. Sample Data — Handle It Like a Professional

Load `sample-catalog.csv` into a scratch ShopCore-shaped schema and **count the dirt before designing anything:**

```sql
-- The first four queries of every data-reality audit:
SELECT count(*) total, count(DISTINCT isbn) distinct_isbn FROM sample_catalog;
SELECT category, count(*) n FROM sample_catalog GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY n DESC LIMIT 10;
SELECT count(*) zero_copies FROM sample_catalog WHERE copies_total = 0;
SELECT count(*) undated FROM sample_catalog WHERE year = 0;
```

Findings drive decisions: e.g., "7% of rows have `copies=0` meaning *unknown* → the catalog must distinguish `0` (genuinely zero copies) from `NULL` (unknown), or Karen can't reconcile." You get these insights *by querying*, never by staring at the CSV.

## 4. What "Done" Looks Like for Stage 1

- [ ] Discovery brief signed off by the client (role-played: by you, honestly)
- [ ] Sample data profiled: row counts, distinct ISBNs, category distribution, anomaly rates
- [ ] Assumptions list written down (each with "client confirmed" or "assumed, validate in Stage 2")
- [ ] Top-3 risks named: data quality, scope creep ("while you're here, add…"), AI trust
