The second most common FDE message — and the one that fails most often when done naively:

> **"We have a manual process eating 20 hours a week. Can you add AI to it?"**

The correct answer is rarely "yes, here's a chatbot". It is almost always **a deterministic pipeline with AI at the edges, human approval in the middle, and an eval suite before anyone calls it done.** Work it in order.

## 1. The Client Request, Verbatim

> "Every Monday our ops team downloads CSV exports from three systems, reconciles ~400 invoices by hand against POs, flags mismatches, and emails approvers. It takes two people 4 hours each. Can you automate this with AI?"

**Delivery constraint:** 3 weeks. No data may leave the building ("everything stays on-prem/VPC"). One engineer, part-time, for the build.

## 2. Discovery Questions — The Written Set

1. **Current steps, verbatim:** walk me through one invoice, start to finish. Where does it enter, who touches it, what do they actually do with it?
2. **The matching rule:** how does a human decide "match" vs "mismatch"? Write it down as rules first — 80% of matching is usually deterministic (PO number + amount ± 1% + vendor name fuzzy-equal).
3. **The exceptions:** what share of the 400 need judgment? Which ones? (This decides whether AI is even needed.)
4. **Output:** who consumes the result, in what format, where does it go? Who approves?
5. **Error cost:** what happens when a wrong flag auto-approves a bad invoice? Who is accountable?
6. **Data:** schemas of the three CSVs; sample files; are PO numbers consistent across systems?
7. **Constraints:** data residency (on-prem/VPC confirmed), budget, who maintains this after you leave?
8. **Success:** what does "done" look like — hours saved? error rate? QBR metric?

**Hidden requirements:**
- "AI" here means "we heard of GPT"; the real ask is **eliminate the 4-hour slog without breaking financial controls**.
- The unmatched 10–15% must route to a human with context, not vanish into a confidence threshold.
- Auditability is non-negotiable (it's financial data): every auto-decision must be explainable and reversible.

## 3. Decompose Into Deterministic vs Probabilistic — Before Choosing Tools

| Step | Deterministic | AI-assisted (justify it) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Parse 3 CSVs, join on PO number | 100% — strict schema validation | Never |
| Exact/near match (PO + amount + vendor fuzzy) | 85–90% of 400 — rules + fuzzy string match | No |
| Amount within tolerance? | 100% deterministic | No |
| Vendor name variants ("Acme Ltd" vs "ACME LIMITED") | Normalize + alias table | Optionally embedding similarity as fallback signal — logged |
| Mismatch reason classification for the exception queue | Keywords → 70% coverage | Yes — classify into `{price, qty, vendor, missing PO, duplicate}` for the human queue |
| Email draft to approver for exceptions | Approved template with slots filled by fields | Yes — draft only, human sends |
| Approve/reject decision | **Never AI.** Human signs. | Never |

This table is the deliverable of discovery step 3 — show it to the client before writing a line of code. Their reaction ("actually 95% of it is rules") tells you the real project size.

## 4. The Build — Layers, Not a Prompt

1. **Deterministic core:** parse + validate + join + match (pure functions, unit-tested). Handles ~85-90% with zero model involvement.
2. **AI edge (only where judgment is genuinely needed):** the exception classifier and the email draft. Small model, structured output (`{category, confidence, rationale}`), temperature 0.
3. **Human approval gate:** the exception queue UI shows the invoice, the PO, the mismatch, the AI's classification + rationale, and Approve/Reject/Needs-info buttons. **Nothing posts, nothing emails, nothing is marked paid without a click.**
4. **Audit log:** every invoice: original values, rule/AI decision, who/what decided, timestamp — one row, immutable.
5. **Eval before go-live:** hand-label 100 historical invoices (50 matched, 50 mismatched + 10 adversarial). Measure precision/recall **of the exception classifier**. Target: ≥ 0.9 precision (false approvals are the expensive error) — measure, don't guess.

## 5. Guardrails That Actually Matter (Not the Prompt Ones)

- AI output is **data, never instructions** — the classifier cannot trigger any action beyond writing a category string.
- **Human signature is the control.** The model cannot mark anything approved, paid, or sent.
- **Scope cap:** the tool may only touch the three CSVs and the exception queue — no email sending, no payment API.
- **Kill switch:** one env var (`RECON_AI_ENABLED=0`) turns every AI step off and routes 100% to the human queue — the process must still work if the model disappears.
- **Idempotency:** re-running the Monday job on the same CSVs must not duplicate queue entries (key = invoice_id).

## 6. Verification You Can Show the Client

```text
Historical backtest: 400 invoices (Q3), blind-run.
Auto-resolved by rules: 341 (85.3%). Correct: 339. False auto-approvals: 0.
Exception queue: 59. AI classification correct: 54/59 (91.5%); 5 misclassified
  → all caught by human review before any payment (as designed).
Estimated time saving: 7.6 hrs/week → ~3.8 hrs/week (this is an estimate,
  labelled as such — true ROI measured in week 2, not promised now).
```

## 7. Rollout and Handover

- **Week 1:** shadow mode — tool runs but everything still goes through human hands; compare outputs.
- **Week 2:** rules-only mode with AI suggestions shown (non-binding).
- **Week 3+:** full flow with human approval active; weekly 30-min review of the exception queue for the first month.
- Handover: runbook (how to reprocess a week, how to rotate the alias table, how to read the audit log), kill-switch location, owner named.

## 8. What This Scenario Teaches About FDE Work

| Skill | Where It Shows Up |
| :-- | :-- |
| Saying "not with AI" where rules win | §3 — the honest scoping table wins trust |
| Human-in-the-loop as architecture | §5 — approval gates are load-bearing, not decoration |
| Measuring before promising | §6 — backtest numbers, labeled estimates |
| Degrading gracefully | §5 kill switch — the business never depends on the model |

**Exercise.** A client asks you to "use AI to answer inbound customer emails end to end." Write your discovery questions (8), your deterministic/AI decomposition table, three guardrails that cannot be overridden, and the eval you would demand before go-live.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your decomposition table assigns at least two steps explicitly to deterministic code, names who approves any money-moving action, and your eval has a numeric pass bar.
