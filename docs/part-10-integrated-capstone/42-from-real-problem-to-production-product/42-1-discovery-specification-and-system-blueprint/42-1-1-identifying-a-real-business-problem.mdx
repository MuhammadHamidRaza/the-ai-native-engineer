Most capstones fail before the first line of code: they pick a topic ("a task app") instead of a problem ("invoices arrive wrong for 15% of clients"). This lesson is the difference between a school project and a professional delivery — starting from a **real, painful, measurable** business problem.

## 1. What Makes a Problem Real

A real problem has four signatures:

1. **Someone is losing money, time, or trust because of it today** — not hypothetically.
2. **The status quo is imperfect** — a workaround exists but bleeds hours or errors.
3. **The pain owner can name a number** — 4 hrs/week, 12% error rate, $300/customer.
4. **A fix is bounded** — solvable in 3–6 weeks by one engineer with known tools.

| Not a problem | Real problem |
| :-- | :-- |
| "People forget their groceries" | "Our Shopify store sends 40% of orders with wrong quantities and nobody notices until the customer complains — 15% return rate, $18k refund cost/quarter." |
| "I want to build an AI assistant" | "Our support agents spend 60% of their time answering 'where is my order' — 22 tickets/day, could be self-serve." |
| "Todo app" | "Freelance designers lose 7 hrs/week reconciling invoices vs bank statements by spreadsheet." |

## 2. Sourcing Real Problems

Ranked by richness for a capstone:

1. **Your own freelance/client work** (best) — a client told you about this last month.
2. **Local business on your street** — ask the cafe owner / dentist / gym manager about their worst repetitive headache.
3. **Your employer's internal tooling** — ask a PM what manual process they'd automate today.
4. **Open-source organizations you admire** — GitHub issues tagged `help wanted`.

**Do not pick**: fictional students' hypothetical problems, problems whose pain you cannot observe, or problems that require enterprise data access you cannot get.

## 3. The Problem Validation Interview (30 min, real humans)

Interview **at least three** people who live with the problem. Questions:

1. Walk me through the last time this happened. *(concrete, not abstract)*
2. How often? What does it cost you — minutes, dollars, stress?
3. What have you tried? Why did it fail?
4. If it worked perfectly, what would change on Monday morning?
5. Who else touches this process? Who decides to buy the fix?

**Reject problems that**: nobody can quantify, nobody can decide, or the workaround is already "fine".

## 4. The Capstone PRD Skeleton (1 page)

```text
PROBLEM: [One sentence, with a number]
CURRENT WORKAROUND: [What they do today — the spreadsheet, the email chain]
WHO FEELS IT: [Role + context — "Shopify merchants with >200 orders/mo"]
SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE: [Observable metric — "wrong-quantity shipments <2%"]
OUT OF SCOPE: [What you will NOT build — keeps the capstone shippable]
```

## 5. Worked Example (From a Real Capstone)

> **Problem**: Brightside Yoga (3 studios, ~400 members) spends 6 hrs/week manually reconciling class bookings vs payment logs because their booking app and their payment processor don't talk. Result: 12% of members are charged for classes they don't attend, 8 complaints/month, 2 memberships cancelled.
>
> **Success metric**: Reconciliation is automated nightly; manual intervention drops to &lt;1 hr/week; zero payment disputes from booking mismatches.

That problem is real (measurable), owned (studio manager), bounded (nightly job + dashboard), and solvable with tools you already know (cron, Stripe API, email alerts).

## 6. Common Failure Modes

- **"AI for X"** — the technology is not the problem. Start with the pain, then ask whether AI helps.
- **Scope creep by default** — if you can't name what's *out of scope*, you don't have a capstone, you have a roadmap.
- **Unverifiable success** — "make it better" is not a metric. "Reduce manual reconciliation from 6hrs → 1hr/week" is.
- **Picking what you want to learn instead of what they need** — the capstone proves you can serve a user, not that you can learn a framework.

## 7. Exercise

Write a one-page Capstone PRD for a problem you can personally verify (own pain, or a real business you can interview). Show it to one other engineer; if they can't restate the problem and the success metric back to you, rewrite it.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- [ ] The problem statement contains at least one number (minutes, dollars, rate).
- [ ] You have interviewed ≥2 people who live with the problem.
- [ ] Success is a measurable, observable metric — not "better" or "faster".
- [ ] Scope fits 3–6 weeks of one engineer's time.
- [ ] The PRD fits on one page.
