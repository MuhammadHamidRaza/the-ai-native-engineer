Chapter 42 is the capstone — the one artifact that proves you can take a messy real-world problem from ambiguity to production without scaffolding underneath you. It is the final integration of every skill taught in Parts 00–09: discovery, specification, implementation, testing, security, operations, and delivery.

**Chapter 42 (`36 Focused Hours`)** is organized around three gates:

- **42.1 Discovery, Specification and System Blueprint** — finding a real, measurable business problem and designing the smallest system that solves it.
- **42.2 Full-Stack and AI Implementation** — shipping the deterministic spine first, then adding agents and MCP only when justified by the problem.
- **42.3 Production Deployment and Launch** — hardening, testing, deploying, handing over, and proving you moved the needle.

## What This Capstone Is Not

- A tutorial project with a prefab scope. You choose the problem; you own the outcome.
- A proof that you can use every tool. It is proof that you can **ship** — the one skill employers and clients actually pay for.
- Complete without a real client or a realistic proxy. If you cannot interview a real person about a real pain, the capstone is incomplete by definition.

## What This Capstone Is

- A 3–6 week sprint from a named problem to a deployed URL the client uses.
- A repeatable delivery method: PRD → schema → slice → test → deploy → handover.
- The artifact you put in front of an interviewer or a client and say: "I built this. It works. Here are the metrics it moved."

## Exit Criteria — You Are Done When You Can Show

- [ ] A deployed URL the client (or a peer reviewer acting as one) can click through
- [ ] A PRD, schema, one working slice end-to-end, and a handover pack
- [ ] At least one guarded, tested AI component — or a written justification for none
- [ ] Evidence the metric you chose actually moved (or an honest "pending, 30 days" note)
- [ ] A postmortem of one production incident you handled (or simulated) during delivery

## How to Work Through This Chapter

Do the lessons in order. Do not write code until your PRD survives two outside readers. Do not deploy until the red-team pass finds zero critical issues. Do not collect your invoice until the client signs the acceptance checklist.

**Prerequisites:** Parts 00–09 complete. Particularly Chapters 03, 06, 07, 12, 13, 15, 21, 27, 28 and 36 — the capstone assumes all of them.
