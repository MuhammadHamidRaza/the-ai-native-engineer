Offers are data points, not validation. The engineers who maximize long-term career value treat an offer as a multi-year balance-sheet decision: comp, growth, learning, prestige, optionality — and they negotiate all of it, not just the base. This lesson teaches the full stack of offer evaluation and negotiation, from the moment the recruiter says "we want to move forward".

## 1. What an Offer Actually Contains (2026 Version)

| Component | What to clarify |
| :-- | :-- |
| Base salary | Cash, predictable |
| Equity (startup) | Strike price, preference stack, vesting (4y/1y cliff), total shares, % on a fully-diluted basis — ask for the numbers in writing |
| Equity (public) | RSUs — real market value, refreshers, vest schedule |
| Bonus | Target %, payout history, when paid |
| Sign-on | One-time, sometimes gross-up |
| Perks | Actual value: healthcare, 401k match, learning budget, hardware, WFH, relocation |

**Total comp math** — compare offers on cash + equity at a *conservative* equity valuation (startup: Model it at $0 or at last round minus 30%; public: current price). Anything fancier is comfort, not math.

## 2. The Five-Axis Scorecard

Score each offer 1–5 on:

1. **Learning** — will I be better in 2 years? (Strongest predictor of long-term comp)
2. **Trajectory** — promotion speed, scope growth, mentor access
3. **Brand/Proof** — will this name help the *next* role?
4. **Stability/Risk** — runway, revenue, market position, team health
5. **Lifestyle** — hours, remote reality, manager quality, on-call

A startup at 5/5/5/2/3 beats big tech at 1/3/4/5/4 for most early-career engineers — and the "wrong" answer for your neighbor is the right answer for you. There is no universal max; there is a personal optimum.

## 3. Negotiating — The Scripts That Work

**First principle: everything is negotiable, including non-comp.** Salary, equity, signing bonus, title, start date, vacation, learning budget, remote days, equipment, relocation — all fair game. Recruiters negotiate these daily; you won't offend anyone by asking politely with data.

**The playbook, in order:**

1. **Get competing offers** (or credible equivalent signals — other final-round loops). This is 80% of the leverage.
2. **Express enthusiasm, then ask for time.** "I'm genuinely excited about this — can I have until Thursday to respond?" (Always do this; never answer on the call.)
3. **Anchor with data.** "Based on levels.fyi and my research on bands for this level + market, I was expecting $X. Can we get there?"
4. **Negotiate holistically.** "Base at $X, and I'll need the signing bonus to cover the unvested equity I'm leaving."
5. **Close gracefully.** "If we can get to $X, I'm ready to sign today." (The strongest close signals genuine intent.)

**What not to do:** bluff a walk-away you won't take · negotiate via email walls of text (a 15-minute call > a novel) · ask for absurd amounts that signal poor calibration · negotiate with anyone but the recruiter (their job is this) · renegotiate after signing (that bridge burns fast).

## 4. Leveling — The Hidden Leverage

**The single most important salary decision is the level you're hired at** — one level up is often 25–40% more, and it compounds on every future raise and offer. Push the *level*, not just the base:

- Bring evidence: your scope in past roles (owned X, led Y, incident-commander Z) framed against their level descriptors.
- Ask: "Which level do you have me at? What evidence would support the next one?" — recruiters often have flexibility at the level boundary.
- A "strong hire" at one level is more valuable than a mid-band at the level above.

## 5. Evaluating Red Flags

| Flag | What it signals |
| :-- | :-- |
| "We're like a family" | Unpaid overtime culture, boundary violations |
| Vague equity, no cap table details | You won't understand dilution until it's too late |
| No written offer | Never real; demand one |
| Manager won't introduce you to the team pre-close | Toxic or chaotic environment |
| Salary below your current + large equity promise | You're being paid in lottery tickets — price the risk |
| "Unlimited PTO" | Often *less* time taken off than accrual systems |
| Pressure to sign today | Legitimate doubts count; if they won't give you 48h, walk |

## 6. Long-Term Leverage — Playing the Decade Game

- **Stack titles, not years**: two promotions in four years beats four years of tenure at one level.
- **Compound visibility**: blog posts, talks, OSS, internal RFCs — your working reputation outlives any single logo.
- **Equity resets matter**: the jump from $20k RSU vest to $80k RSU vest at a new company is a bigger life event than a $15k raise at your current one. Review every 2–3 years honestly.
- **Know your market value**: one external interview per year, even if you're happy — the market is your truth serum.
- **Stay or go by the curve**: leave when the learning plateaus, not when the frustration peaks.

## 7. Exercise

Score two hypothetical offers on the five-axis scorecard (or your two real final-round offers), build the negotiation plan for the one you'd prefer, including your anchor, competing-leverage, and which non-comp items you'd trade. Write the exact email/call script. Practice it once out loud with a friend — delivery matters as much as content.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your plan names: anchor number with a source, one non-comp item, the close ("ready to sign today"), and a decision deadline. Your scorecard compares at least two options on all five axes with honest weights.
