Buyers don't hire engineers; they hire **low-risk bets**. Your profiles — LinkedIn, Upwork, portfolio, email signature — are the evidence buyers scan in 90 seconds to decide if you're a safe bet. This lesson turns each surface into a trust instrument for one specific buyer.

## 1. The Buyer's 90-Second Scan (Know It or Lose It)

On your profile, in order: (1) headline — who it's for and what they get; (2) photo/face — human trust; (3) recent post or pinned project — proof; (4) recommendations — social validation; (5) contact path — one click. Missing any → bounce.

## 2. Channel-Specific Positioning

| Channel | Headline formula | Pinned proof |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| LinkedIn | "I help [buyer] get [outcome] with [stack]" | Repo + case study post |
| Upwork | Niche title "Shopify Checkout Performance Consultant" | Portfolio with measured deltas |
| Contra | Role + 3 portfolio works | Deep case studies |
| Portfolio site | One-line positioning + 3 case studies | Video/readme |

**Consistency rule:** same one-line positioning on all five. Scattered positioning reads as scattered craft.

## 3. The Proof Stack (Order of Impact)

1. **Measured result on a real project** — "p95 480→90ms" beats 500 stars.
2. **Named testimonial** — one client quote with initials + context.
3. **Open-source artifact** — repo with clean README, tests, license.
4. **Content** — one deep post showing how you think.
5. **Speaking/community** — talks, mentors, OSS reviews.

If you have zero of these, manufacture #3 today: take your best side project, write a proper README, add tests, push it, and pin it. A week of polishing one repo outperforms a month of generic networking.

## 4. The Recommendation Engine

Ask mid-engagement, at the win moment: "This went well — would you be open to a 2-sentence LinkedIn recommendation about the outcome, not the effort?" Give them the easy path: three bullets they can reuse. Every happy client is a reference you haven't asked for yet.

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Headline says "Available for work"** — buyers see every freelancer saying that; it says nothing.
- **No face / no name / no geography** — buyers hire people; anonymity reads as risk.
- **Portfolio of five mediocre projects** — cut to three great ones.
- **Generic endorsements ("great professional!")** — worthless; ask for outcome-specific quotes.

## 6. Exercise

Rewrite your LinkedIn headline, your Upwork title, and your portfolio one-liner to the same six-word positioning. Pin your best three proof items. Request two recommendations this week using the bullets-template approach.

**Acceptance criteria.** A stranger in your niche can state in one sentence what you do, for whom, and the proof you carry. All three surfaces match.
