Buyers don't believe claims; they believe **stories with receipts**. A case study that names the problem, the constraint, the approach, and a measured result is the single most persuasive artifact you will ever produce. This lesson teaches the case-study format that closes clients and the portfolio that frames them.

## 1. The Case-Study Template Buyers Trust

```markdown
# HelpBot: Deflecting 62% of Tier-1 Support for ShopCore

**Client:** ShopCore (e-commerce, 40k orders/yr) — scoped to two ops pilots
**Role:** Solo consultant — AI/RAG implementation
**Timeline:** 6 weeks · **Stack:** Next.js, Postgres + pgvector, OpenAI API, Vercel

## Problem
Support agents spent ~60% of their time on refund/warranty questions already
answered in the policy docs. Errors were common (wrong refund citations).

## Constraints
Pilot had to ship without touching the CRM or auto-responding (human-in-loop only).
Existing policies were PDFs with inconsistent headings.

## Approach
PDF ingestion → chunking (500 tokens) → pgvector embeddings → hybrid search
(RRF) → answer with citations + confidence threshold; below-threshold → handoff.
Counted deflections as "agent-sent no reply, AI answer marked correct".

## Results
- 62% of tier-1 tickets deflected after 4 weeks
- Refund citation accuracy: 91% → 99%
- p95 answer latency: 1.4s
- $0 infra cost beyond API; pilot payback modeled at 3 months

## What I'd Do Next
Quarterly eval of refusal correctness; add order-lookup tool with approval gate.
```

Notice: every line is about **their** problem with **your** contribution framed narrowly. Not "we built a cool pipeline" — "their refund error rate dropped 40%; here's the mechanism".

## 2. Three Case Studies That Cover the Buyer's Risk Model

1. **Speed win** — a 48h/72h rescue. Shows you deliver fast under pressure.
2. **Scale win** — latency, throughput, cost reduction at volume. Shows you think about systems.
3. **Judgment win** — you talked them out of an over-built solution and saved money. Shows you're an advisor, not just an implementer.

If you only have one, build the other two from your own side projects with honest labels ("personal project, simulated scale") — honest framing beats implied client work.

## 3. The Proof-Driven Portfolio Structure

```markdown
# Hamid Raza — AI-native engineer

Helping ShopCore-scale businesses ship AI features that survive production.
Fastify · PostgreSQL · Next.js · OpenAI/Claude APIs

## Selected work
1. HelpBot RAG — case study (deflected 62% tier-1 volume)
2. Billing idempotency migration — case study ($0 double-charges, 100% replay safety)
3. API latency rescue — case study (p95 480ms → 90ms)

## Writing
- Why your RAG eval matters more than your model (blog post)
- The idempotency-key pattern for Stripe retries (blog post)

## Contact
hello@hamidraza.dev · calendar.app/hamid · github.com/hamidraza
```

Rule: **three case studies, two posts, one calendar link**. Everything else is decoration.

## 4. Getting Permission & Numbers

- Ask clients for permission to share — most say yes with light anonymization ("a 9-figure Shopify merchant in NYC").
- If they refuse, publish the work as "anonymous e-commerce retailer, details on request" — still credible.
- Numbers beat adjectives. If you have none, instrument before/after now: latency via `curl -w`, deflection via a log query, cost via API dashboard exports.

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Tech-stack selfie** — "Built with Next.js + pgvector + OpenAI + Redis + Docker + …" as if stacks close deals. They don't. Outcomes close deals.
- **No before/after** — "improved performance" without numbers reads as vibes.
- **Five weak case studies** — three strong ones beat five thin ones every time.
- **Hiding the failure modes** — "here's what went wrong and how we fixed it" is the most trusted paragraph in the document.

## 6. Exercise

Write one complete case study from a past project using the markdown template above, get a peer to read it cold, and fix the three unclear points they flag. Publish it on your site or as a public Gist; pin it on LinkedIn with a 3-sentence intro.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your case study names: a specific client type, one measured before/after number, one constraint, one honest "what went wrong" line, and a CTA to talk.
