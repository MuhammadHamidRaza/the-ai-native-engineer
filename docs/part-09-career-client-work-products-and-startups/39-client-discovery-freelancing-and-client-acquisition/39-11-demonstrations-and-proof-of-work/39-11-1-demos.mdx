Proof beats promise every time — but a bad demo sinks trust it took weeks to build. This lesson teaches you to run lean, 10-minute technical demos that make the economic case obvious.

## 1. The Demo Charter

10 minutes. One question. One outcome. No slides (or max 2). Every minute must answer: "why does this matter to my business?"

## 2. The Demo Script

1. **Context (30s)** — restate their problem in your words.
2. **Show (7 min)** — live code/walkthrough; narrate decisions, not syntax.
3. **Pause for reactions (2 min)** — let them ask; answer honestly or say "I'll check".
4. **Trade-offs (1 min)** — what this doesn't do yet; you're credible when you admit gaps.
5. **Next step (30s)** — clear action: "I'll send the eval report Friday."

## 3. Live vs Recorded

- **Live** — high trust, riskier (network, typos); use only if stable.
- **Recorded Loom** — repeatable, safe; link with timestamps.
- **Hybrid** — short live demo + Loom for replay.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Showing everything** — audience decides nothing; narrow the lens.
- **Reading code aloud** — nobody learns syntax in 7 min; narrate outcomes.
- **No error path shown** — admitting a failure mode gets you instant credibility.
- **No fallback if the network dies** — always record or screen-capture; "my wifi died" kills more demos than bad code.
- **Demo without a wall-clock** — set a phone timer visibly; a 25-minute demo of a 10-minute slot loses the room.

## 5. Worked Demo (CareQueue, Dana the Office Manager)

A buyer-side walkthrough — every step either earns trust or spends it.

```text
[0:00] "Dana, when I asked about your morning last week, you said the billing
       inbox costs you ~45 minutes. Here's your 8:15am, one week ago —
       63 messages, flagged by urgency."

[0:40] (Screen: inbox sorted into Billing / Scheduling / Admin / Needs Human)
       "Watch the top-left: the two billing questions in red were sitting
       unanswered for 2 days. Those are the patients who call angry."

[1:30] "Open this one — 'why was I charged twice?' The system found the
       duplicate charge (order #4471 and #4489 same amount, same card) and
       drafted this reply using your practice's refund policy, which I digested
       from your PDF. Feel free to read it."

[4:00] "That draft — approve, edit, or reject. One click. Cost so far:
       45 seconds of your morning instead of 45 minutes."

[6:00] "Honest limit: it can't see insurance EOBs yet — those still need you.
       Also, one in ten drafts cites the wrong policy line; you catch those;
       I retrain weekly. Here's the rejection log from the pilot."

[8:30] "Next step, if this looks useful: a 2-week pilot on your real inbox,
       free. I'll sit in your front desk with you Monday 8am."
```

**What this demo proves, minute by minute:** answers their stated pain (0:00), shows the money outcome (0:40), proves the value with their own data (1:30), demonstrates the exact interaction (4:00), establishes credibility with limits + pilot data (6:00), ends with a concrete ask (8:30). Nothing in it is generic.

### Pre-Demo Checklist

- [ ] Their data is visible, not lorem-ipsum test records — realism is the product.
- [ ] One happy path + one honest failure/limitation, rehearsed twice.
- [ ] Timer in view; recorded fallback ready (screen capture before the call).
- [ ] You know the 3 questions they will ask, and your honest answer to each.
- [ ] One specific, small, dated next step — never "I'll follow up".

## 6. Exercise

Record a 10-minute demo of CareQueue (or your own product) using the § 5 structure. Show it to one non-technical person; ask them to answer, from memory: what does this do, what doesn't it do yet, what's the next step?

**Acceptance criteria.** Your demo covers their problem, one working beat using their (or realistic) data, one honest limitation with evidence, and one clear next step with a date — ≤10 minutes, timer visible, recorded fallback confirmed.
