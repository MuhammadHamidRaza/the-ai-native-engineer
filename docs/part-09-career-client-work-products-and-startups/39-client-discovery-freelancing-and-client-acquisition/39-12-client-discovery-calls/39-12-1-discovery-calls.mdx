Discovery calls are where deals live or die — not because of your pitch, but because of how well you listen. This lesson teaches the question framework that surfaces budget, authority, urgency, and real pain while building trust instead of tension.

## 1. The Discovery Flow

1. **Goodwill opener (2 min)** — thank them, set agenda.
2. **Their world (10 min)** — goals, current workflow, frustrations (open questions: "walk me through how X works today").
3. **Pain quantification (5 min)** — "what does this cost you weekly?"
4. **Aspirational frame (2 min)** — "what would 'fixed' look like?"
5. **Budget/timing (3 min)** — gentle, direct.
6. **Next steps (3 min)** — clear action, date, who does what.

## 2. The Question Bank

- "What's working today? What isn't?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand, what would change first?"
- "Who else is affected by this problem?"
- "What have you tried? What failed?"
- "What would make this a 10/10 project?"
- "Is there budget allocated, or are you exploring?"

## 3. The Reverse Pitch (When They Ask What You Do)

Describe in 3 sentences; tie to their pain: "I help practices like yours automate billing-inbox triage so your office manager gets her mornings back. Last month a 4-chair practice cut billing questions from 45 minutes to 12. Want the 10-minute Loom?"

## 3.5 A Complete Annotated Discovery Transcript (Brightside Dental)

```text
[0:00] You:  Dana, thanks for the 20 minutes. Here's my plan: 10 min on
       your world, 5 on what "fixed" looks like, then numbers. Sound good?
[0:20] Dana: Sure.
[0:30] You:  Walk me through a normal Tuesday 8am.
[0:40] Dana: I open Google, 63 emails. Half are billing. I paste them into
       ChatGPT by hand. By 9:30 I'm behind on patients.
[2:10] You:  What does that cost you? [NOT "Would this save you time?"]
[2:20] Dana: Honestly? 45 minutes a day, maybe an hour. And two angry-patient
       calls a week.
[3:00] You:  What have you tried?
[3:10] Dana: Canned Outlook replies. My VA handled it but missed insurance
       every time. We let him go.
[4:30] You:  If this inbox were simply not your problem anymore, what
       changes on Monday morning?
[4:40] Dana: I'd open the inbox and see it already sorted. Billing ones
       drafted, I just approve. I'd actually see my patients on time.
[6:30] You:  Two questions so I don't waste your time: is there a budget
       this quarter, and who else needs to sign off?
[6:40] Dana: Budget's approved up to ~$10k this quarter, Dr. Reyes (owner)
       needs to say yes.
[8:00] You:  Great. Here's what I propose — a 2-week pilot on your real
       inbox, $0, I sit in with you week one. End of week 2 you decide:
       roll out or I walk away. I'll send a one-pager today.
[9:00] Dana: That works. Send it over.
[9:10] You:  Done — I'll email it by 5pm with three slots for next week.
       Anything I missed about your schedule?
```

**Annotations:** (2:10) quantifies pain in their words, not yours — $30/hr × 45 min ≈ $700/mo, useful later in the proposal; (3:10) past failure = the exact gap you fill; (4:40) their desired outcome in their words — this sentence becomes your proposal headline; (6:30) budget + authority surfaced without pressure; (8:00) pilot framed as zero-risk decision with a date; (9:10) single concrete next step with a deadline. **You never said what you build; you asked, listened, and confirmed fit.**

### The 5 Questions That Unlock Every Discovery

1. "Walk me through a normal [day/week/process]." — baseline, costs, frequency
2. "What have you tried? Why did it fail?" — the gap you fill
3. "What does this cost you now?" — time, money, reputation (in their words)
4. "If this were fixed, what Monday morning looks like?" — success vision
5. "Who else decides? Is there budget? By when?" — authority + urgency

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Pitching in minute 3** — you'll lose them; ask first.
- **No budget/urgency discovery** — wasting your time and theirs.
- **No clear next step** — deals stall; always end with date + action.

## 5. Exercise

Role-play one 20-minute discovery call with a peer; record or note answers. Debrief: did you surface budget, authority, timeline?

**Acceptance criteria.** Your call covers their goal, pain, budget hint, and a dated next step; no pitching before minute 10.
