The three questions that determine whether a lead is real: what needs doing, by when, and who pays. This lesson teaches you to extract these honestly and structure them into a scope you can price and deliver.

## 1. Scope Extraction Technique

"Let's map the smallest fix that moves the number." Then:
- What is included? (features, data sources, integrations)
- What is explicitly out? (auth, scale, non-goals)
- What does 'done' look like? (demoable outcome, not "working on it")

## 2. Urgency vs Budget Matrix

| Urgency \\ Budget | Low | Mid | High |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| High | Escalate or narrow scope | Fast engagement | Ideal |
| Mid | Qualify arduously | Normal | Normal |
| Low | Nurture forever | Low priority | Rare gem |

## 3. Budget Probing Without Awkwardness

- "What's the investment range you had in mind?"
- "Typical engagements in this space run $X–$Y; does that align with your planning?"
- "Outside that range, I can tell you now where we'd land."

## 4. The Scope-of-Work Snapshot

Write it live or same-day: goals, in-scope, out-of-scope, timeline, price, payment terms, acceptance criteria. Get verbal yes on the snapshot before building anything.

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Scope ambiguity** — you pay for their vagueness forever.
- **Ignoring budget** — building enthusiasm on air.
- **No acceptance criteria** — endless "one more thing" loop.

## 6. Exercise

From a real or mock discovery call, draft a one-page scope-of-work snapshot. Verify it covers goals, in/out, timeline, price, terms, acceptance.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your snapshot: ≤1 page, explicit exclusions, dated delivery, named acceptance test.
