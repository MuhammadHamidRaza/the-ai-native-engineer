Price too low, you attract the worst clients; too high, you attract suspicious silence. This lesson teaches value-based pricing as the default — with hourly and milestone as valid fallbacks — and how to hold your number when they flinch.

## 1. The Three Pricing Models

| Model | When to use | Risk |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Hourly** | Unknown scope, advisory, red-team | You subsidize their indecision |
| **Fixed-price / milestone** | Defined deliverable (audit, integration) | Scope creep — mitigate with exclusions |
| **Value-based** | Quantified outcome (latency, deflection, revenue) | Requires trust + measurement plan |

**Default rule:** start with fixed/milestone; escalate to value once they trust you.

## 2. Value Pricing Formula

```
Price ≈ (their annual cost of pain) × (fraction you capture)
```

Example: ticket deflection saves $100k/yr → $15k-30k is rational for you. Show the math; they respect logic over adjectives.

## 3. Anchoring & Negotiation

- Anchor **high** with justification (comparable project, cost of inaction).
- Don't discount; trade scope ("drop the eval suite → price drops 20%").
- Every dollar off your rate teaches them to negotiate everything.

## 4. The Honesty Principle

If scope is unknown, don't value-price — hourly with a cap or a paid discovery phase. Pretending certainty destroys trust.

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Underpricing** — attracting hagglers; you become cheap, not in-demand.
- **No exclusions** — scope creep eats margin silently.
- **Time-based guilt** — billing hours feels weird; shift to outcome framing.

## 6. Exercise

Pick a past project; price it three ways (hourly, fixed, value). Which would you charge? Write your anchor statement.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your pricing rationale: one sentence on why this model, one number justified by their cost of pain.
