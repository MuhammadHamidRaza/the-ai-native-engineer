Most freelance proposals are thin capability lists that read like homework. A professional proposal is a **business case for their money** — problem, outcome, approach, price, terms, next step. This lesson teaches the structure that wins.

## 1. The Proposal Skeleton

1. **Restate their problem** — their words; they feel heard.
2. **Outcome** — measurable target; not "features".
3. **Approach** — phased; 3-5 steps; not tech-stack salad.
4. **Investment** — fixed price or milestones; include terms (net-14/30, deposit %).
5. **Timeline** — start + delivery; milestones with dates.
6. **Risk & mitigation** — show you've thought; builds confidence.
7. **Next step** — "sign by Friday; I start Monday." Calendar link.

## 2. One-Page Proposal Template (CareQueue @ Brightside Dental)

```text
PROJECT: CareQueue for Brightside Dental — inbox triage for front desk

YOUR WORDS (from our call, Dec 4):
"63 emails Monday morning, half billing, ~45 minutes gone before I see
 patients. My VA missed the insurance detail every time."

OUTCOME (we'll know it worked):
- ≥60% of billing questions handled by auto-drafted replies (Dana approves)
- Dana's morning inbox review ≤ 25 minutes (from ~60) by day 45
- Zero patient data leaves your Google Workspace tenant

APPROACH
- Week 1–2: baseline week (I triage with you, learn your tone) + Gmail connect
- Week 3–4: labels live, billing drafts + one-click approve, Marisol training
- Week 5: 30-day review: numbers, case study interview, decide next step

INVEST: $8,000 fixed
  - 50% ($4,000) on signature — net-14
  - $2,000 at milestone 1 (labels + drafts live, 14-day acceptance window)
  - $2,000 at milestone 3 (30-day review + handoff)

TIMELINE
- Kickoff Mon Dec 15, 2026
- M1 demo Fri Jan 9; M3 review Fri Feb 6
- Silence after 14 days on any milestone = accepted

RISK & MITIGATION
- Policy docs stale/incomplete → 2-hour doc triage workshop before build (Day 1, included)
- Dana's tone inconsistent across examples → 10 real replies calibrate the prompt (Week 2)
- Insurance EOBs out of scope → named explicitly; manual handling until Phase 2
- If it doesn't cut her triage time by ≥30 min/day by day 45, milestone 3 is free

NEXT
- Reply "go" by Fri Dec 12 and I'll send the calendar link for kickoff.
- If you need it smaller: we can scope to billing-only for $5,500.

[Name] — [one-line credential] — [calendar link]
```

**Anatomy of why this wins:** (1) their words — they trust what they already believe; (2) one measurable outcome with a date; (3) phased, concrete steps with deliverables; (4) price with terms and a risk reversal ("milestone 3 is free"); (5) timeline with named acceptance semantics; (6) explicit risks — they know what you don't know; (7) one small dated next step. Six of seven elements are *about them*; zero are about you.

## 3. Tailor Every Line

No generic templates. Every sentence references their business, their data, their deadline. Boilerplate is visible and fatal.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Feature dumping** — they buy outcomes, not APIs.
- **Vague timeline** — "soon" = never; dates create commitment.
- **No risk section** — naive = untrustworthy.

## 5. Exercise

Draft a one-page proposal for a mock prospect. Read it aloud; cut anything that doesn't serve their decision.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your proposal: ≤1 page, measurable outcome, phased approach, fixed price, clear next step with date.
