White-labeling is an agency partnership with the volume dial turned up — you're invisible to the end client, the agency owns the relationship, and you deliver like their internal team. It's not for every engineer; it's for engineers who want predictable income without sales.

## 1. When White-Label Fits (and Doesn't)

| Fit | Don't fit |
| :-- | :-- |
| You value steady income over brand | You want public case studies |
| Your niche maps to agency offerings | You want direct client relationships |
| You communicate in agency style (fast, templated) | You hate process or report structure |

## 2. The White-Label Operating Model

- You deliver through the agency's systems: their PM tool, their Slack, their client SLA.
- You never contact the end client without the agency's blessing.
- Your name never appears in deliverables; your code appears in their repo.
- Weekly status goes through the agency.

## 3. Finding White-Label Work

Agency job boards (Remote OK "subcontractor"), Clutch agency listings (email the smaller ones), Upwork agencies looking for "subcontractor", partnerships from Section 39.6.4.

## 4. Protect Yourself

- Written agreement: scope, IP transfer at final payment, rate, payment terms.
- Keep your own portfolio copies allowed in contract (most agencies allow generic case studies).
- Payment guarantee: 50% upfront or milestone-based.
- Exit clause: 30-day notice, no long lock-in.

## 5. Exercise

Draft a 1-page white-label services sheet for your niche (what you deliver, in agency language, without your branding). Send it to 3 agencies you shortlisted.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your sheet uses agency voice ("we deliver white-labeled X for agencies needing Y"), lists 3 service bullets, and includes your subcontractor agreement skeleton.
