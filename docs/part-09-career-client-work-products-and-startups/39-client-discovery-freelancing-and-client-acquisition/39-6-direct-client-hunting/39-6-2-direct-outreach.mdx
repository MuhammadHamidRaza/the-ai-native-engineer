Direct outreach is cold email's human sibling — DMs, comments, LinkedIn messages, and in-person conversations with specific buyers. The channel changes; the discipline doesn't: research, relevance, restraint, and a tracked pipeline.

## 1. The Outreach Ladder (Highest to Lowest Trust)

1. **Comments on their content** — pure value, no pitch. Builds name recognition before any ask.
2. **Thoughtful replies to their posts/articles** — shows you actually read.
3. **DM after engagement** — context already established; warm open.
4. **Cold DM with specific hook** — works with research, dies without it.
5. **Cold calls** — only after 2–3 touches; never first.

Start at the top; descend only as trust permits.

## 2. The Outreach Message Framework

Every message, any channel, answers three questions in ≤100 words:

1. **Who are you?** (one line, relevant)
2. **Why them?** (specific observation from their work)
3. **What's the smallest useful next step?** (15-min call? a resource? just a question?)

```text
Hi Priya — your thread on idempotent Stripe retries saved me last week.
I'm Hamid — full-stack/AI engineer. Not selling — I'm curious how you
handled the webhook replay edge case in your March incident.
```

Notice the shape: admiration → identification → one question. No links, no attachments, no "we".

## 3. The Commenting Strategy (Your Stealth Engine)

Five valuable comments per week on your ideal buyers' content compounds faster than any cold channel. The rule: **comment must be so good the buyer wants to reply** — add data, challenge a premise politely, or share a war story. "Great post!" is invisible.

## 4. Tracking Discipline

Spreadsheet or CRM: name, company, channel, date, touch #, hook, response, next step. 100 rows teaches you which hooks land. No tracking = no compounding learning.

## 5. Volume Targets

20 touches/day (10 comments, 5 DMs, 5 follow-ups) → 10–15% reply → 2–3 calls/week. Burnout comes from intensity, not volume — spread it across your calendar.

## 6. Common Failure Modes

- **Pitching in the DM** — the #1 trust killer; ask first, pitch later (if ever).
- **Generic hooks** — identical copy sent to 50 people; detected instantly.
- **No follow-up** — one touch rarely suffices; sequence of 3 max.
- **Skipping the pipeline** — no tracking means no learning, no scaling.

## 7. Exercise

Build a 50-target list in your niche. Engage with 5 of their posts this week with substantive comments, then DM 3 of the most responsive. Log everything. Reflect: which comment got a reply and why?

**Acceptance criteria.** Your log shows ≥50 targets, ≥5 substantial comments, ≥3 personalized DMs, and one real conversation scheduled.
