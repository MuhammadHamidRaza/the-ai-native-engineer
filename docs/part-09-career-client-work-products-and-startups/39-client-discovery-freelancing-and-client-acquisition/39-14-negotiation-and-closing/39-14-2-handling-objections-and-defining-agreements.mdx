Objections are usually polite requests for more information, not rejection. This lesson teaches you to turn "it's too much/too risky/too soon" into a signed, explicit agreement that protects both sides.

## 1. Objection Patterns & Responses

| Objection | Real meaning | Response |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| "Too expensive" | Value unclear | ROI math: cost of their pain vs price |
| "Not the right time" | No urgency yet | Quantify delay cost; offer smaller audit |
| "We need to think" | Risk fear | Case studies; guarantee; references |
| "We need other bids" | Procurement process | Provide referenceable case studies; fast follow-up |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | Testing your floor | Trade scope, not rate |

## 2. The Agreement Skeleton

Scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, timeline, price + terms (deposit, milestones, net-14/30), IP transfer (on payment), confidentiality, warranty/bugfix window, termination clause. Keep it human-readable — legal review if >$10k.

## 3. The 50% Deposit Rule

Never start without ≥25-50% upfront. It filters tire-kickers and funds your effort.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Conceding rate on first objection** — they'll push harder next.
- **No written agreement** — scope creep is pre-programmed.
- **Starting before deposit clears** — emotional investment with zero leverage.
