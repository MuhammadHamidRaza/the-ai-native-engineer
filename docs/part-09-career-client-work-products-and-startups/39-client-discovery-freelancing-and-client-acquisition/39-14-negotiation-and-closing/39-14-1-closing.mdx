Most deals die not from price but from lack of a clear, confident close. This lesson teaches you to guide a ready buyer across the line — without pressure, tricks, or desperation.

## 1. The Close Flow

1. **Summary** — restate pain, outcome, proposal (1 min).
2. **Silence** — let them think; resist filling it.
3. **Objection surface** — invite concerns ("what's your hesitation?").
4. **Assumptive close** — "so we'll do kickoff Tuesday?" — a gentle assumption is often all that's needed.
5. **Decision deadline** — "I can hold the slot if you sign by Friday."

## 2. Handling the "Too Expensive" Moment

Don't discount — trade. "We can reduce scope: drop the eval suite for now → $7,000 instead of $8,000." Or offer payment terms: "50% now, 50% on acceptance." Price anchors value; price cuts signal doubt.

### An Objection Transcript (Real Shape)

```text
Buyer: "Eight thousand feels like a lot for a two-week pilot."
You:   "Fair — let me see if we're comparing the same numbers. You told me
        the billing inbox costs your office manager ~45 minutes a day.
        At $30/hr loaded, that's about $900/month — $5,400 a year. The
        pilot saves most of that, plus the angry-patient calls that turn
        into lost cases. If the pilot doesn't save your office manager at
        least half of that time, I'll refund the second milestone."
Buyer: "...a performance gate. I can do that."
You:   "One condition: we need the milestone demo recorded so both of us can
        agree on the number next to it. And the slot holds until Friday —
        after that I have two other practices queued behind yours."
```

**What happened:** reframe (cost of waiting), de-risk (refund gate), benign urgency (slot), no price cut. The buyer's job was to say yes to value; you made it easy.

### If They Ask "Can You Do $6,000?"

"I can't deliver the same scope for $6,000 — but I can shrink scope: drop the eval suite and the second training session → $6,500. Or $8,000 as quoted with both. Which fits better?" **Trade, never discount.**

## 3. The Alternative Close (When They Hesitate)

"If timing is off, we can push to March or keep the door open for a smaller audit first." Preserves relationship without losing face.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Fear of the ask** — you built the proposal; they expect to say yes or no; ask.
- **Mystery silence after proposal** — follow up at 3-5 days with one question ("Any questions on the timeline in § 2?").
- **Making it about you** — keep it about their outcome.
- **Papering over an objection with a discount** — buys a yes from a buyer who doesn't value it, and loses price integrity on every future deal.
- **No tie-down** — every yes deserves one confirming question ("So we kickoff Tuesday the 1st?").

## 5. Exercise

Role-play a close conversation with a peer playing a hesitant buyer. Practice three closes: direct, alternative, deadline. Record it; review where you rushed, apologized, or discounted.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your close: restated pain with their number, an invitation for objection handled without discounting, one direct ask with a date, one explicit condition ("slot holds until Friday"). No price cut offered at any point in the recording.
