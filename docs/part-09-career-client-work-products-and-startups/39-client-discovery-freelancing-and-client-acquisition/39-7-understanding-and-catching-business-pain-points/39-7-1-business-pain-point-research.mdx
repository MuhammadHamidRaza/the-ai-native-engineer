Every business has pain; very few have someone listening. This lesson teaches how to surface pain points cheaply and systematically — before you pitch, before you scope, before you invest. The engineer who can see pain others miss wins the engagement.

## 1. The Pain-Finding Playbook

1. **Job postings as pain maps** — "hiring platform engineer" = scaling crisis; "looking for automation help" = manual ops bleeding hours.
2. **Support/review scraping** — G2, Trustpilot, app store reviews; repeated complaints = unmet needs.
3. **Postmortems & outage reports** — public engineering blogs reveal what breaks.
4. **RFP/tender language** — "we need to migrate from X by December" = urgency + budget.
5. **Reddit/forums** — r/shopify "checkout broken"; r/smallbusiness "manual invoicing"; raw pain in plain words.
6. **LinkedIn hiring posts + pain comments** — "struggling with Stripe webhooks" under a job ad = live problem.
7. **Your existing clients** — they tell you what broke this month. Write it down.

## 2. The Pain Qualification Lens

| Question | Signal |
| :-- | :-- |
| Is it urgent? | "by Q3" beats "someday" |
| Is it visible? | Someone loses money or sleep |
| Is it ownable? | Someone has budget authority |
| Is it recurring? | Weekly pain beats once-a-year |
| Is it fixable? | Within your stack and 30-60 days |

Score 1–5 each; ≥18 = pursuit-worthy.

## 3. The Research Brief

For each target, write a one-page brief: company, product, stack (guess via Wappalyzer), recent incident/post, pain hypothesis, 2 hooks. This brief is your outreach foundation.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Solving a pain nobody feels** — you built what you'd want, not what they need.
- **Pain ≠ task** — "fix the API" is a task; "reduce checkout abandonment caused by slow APIs" is a pain.
- **Ignoring budget authority** — the engineer who suffers may not be the one who pays.

## 5. Exercise

Pick 3 companies in your niche. Write a one-page pain brief for each with ≥2 concrete hooks (postmortem, job ad, review quote). Rank by qualification score.

**Acceptance criteria.** Each brief contains a verbatim pain quote from the buyer's world, a budget-holder guess, and a specific 15-min ask for the first conversation.
