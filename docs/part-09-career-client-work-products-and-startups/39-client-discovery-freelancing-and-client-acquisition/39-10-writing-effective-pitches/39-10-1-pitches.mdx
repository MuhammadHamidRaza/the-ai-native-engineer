A pitch is not a capability summary; it's a **prediction about their business** that only you can see. The engineer who says "here's what I see in your stack" will always beat the one who lists frameworks. This lesson builds the pitch structure that converts warmth into calls.

## 1. The 3-Line Pitch

1. **Observation** — one specific thing you noticed (public or from a call).
2. **Prediction** — what it implies ("your checkout latency is why 40% are abandoning").
3. **Offer** — one concrete, low-commitment next step.

```text
"I saw your ShopCore status page — 500ms checkout spikes since the May release.
I suspect it's the N+1 in the cart-total calculation path (seen it thrice).
I can run a 60-min remote pairing session to isolate it for free — if I miss,
no cost; if I'm right, we discuss a fix under NDA."
```

## 2. The Order Call (Context → Crunch → Close)

**Context** — restate the problem in their words. **Crunch** — the real cost of inaction ("$12k/mo leak"). **Close** — clear action, timebox, scope. Never pitch twice; pitch once as an experiment, once as an offer.

## 3. Tailoring by Persona

| Persona | Hook | Close |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Founder | Revenue / runway | "Want me to spend 2 hours proving it out?" |
| VP Eng | Reliability / on-call | "I can have the fix behind a flag in a week" |
| Ops Lead | Hours saved / error rate | "I'll automate this step for you inside your stack" |

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Capability pitch** ("I build Next.js apps") — zero signal; speaks to nobody.
- **Solution before problem** — they hear "something I need" not "something that helps".
- **No cost of inaction** — urgency is the fuel; quantify it.

## 5. Exercise

Write 3 pitches for the same product — founder, VP Eng, Ops Lead — in their own words. Read each aloud; the one that feels honest is your pitch.

**Acceptance criteria.** Each pitch: ≤80 words, one observation, one prediction, one close. No tech stack lists.
