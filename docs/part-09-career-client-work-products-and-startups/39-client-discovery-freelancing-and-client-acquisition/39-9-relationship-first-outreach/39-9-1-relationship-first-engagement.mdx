Every high-ticket freelance engagement started with trust built before the pitch. Relationship-first engagement is the art of becoming known, liked, and (eventually) trusted — so the sale is almost incidental.

## 1. The Trust Ladder

1. **Visible** — they know your name exists (comment, post, mention).
2. **Valued** — they've read something useful you wrote or heard you speak.
3. **Liked** — they enjoyed interacting with you (call, thread, event).
4. **Trusted** — they believe you can solve their problem (case studies, referrals).
5. **Paid** — transaction.

Most engineers skip to 5 and wonder why doors close.

## 2. The Visibility-to-Trust Playbook

- **Comment generously** on your ideal buyers' posts (5/week, substance over flattery).
- **Share your own war stories** — the mistake you made, the fix, the lesson.
- **Send unprompted value** — "saw your postmortem; here's the pattern that would have caught it earlier" — no ask attached.
- **Remember details** — follow up on their known situation ("how did the Q3 launch go?").
- **Public generosity** — amplify their work, tag them, recommend their products.

## 3. The 30-Day Trust Sprint

Week 1: 10 comments, 1 share, 2 likes. Week 2: 1 DM with a resource (no ask). Week 3: mention them in a post. Week 4: 15-min call request.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Transactional from line 1** — asking for a meeting before any value given.
- **Generic compliments** — "great post!" is invisible spam.
- **Ignoring their world** — sending irrelevant resources loses trust fast.

## 5. Exercise

Pick 5 target buyers. Execute the 30-day sprint on 2 of them: log every touch, note reply quality. Reflect: which comment got traction and why?

**Acceptance criteria.** Your log shows ≥15 logged interactions across 5 targets with 2 calls booked by week 4.
