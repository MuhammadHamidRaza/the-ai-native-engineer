Generalists compete on price; specialists compete on judgment. "Full-stack developer" is a resume headline, not a positioning — it tells a buyer nothing about why they should trust you with their specific problem. This lesson teaches you to pick a lucrative, boring-enough-to-win-at niche using five filters — before you invest a month in one.

## 1. The Five-Filter Niche Test

Score each candidate niche 0–2 on five axes. Only proceed if it scores ≥7:

| Filter | Question | 0 | 2 |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Pain density** | Do these buyers have urgent, recurring technical pain? | Nice-to-have | Missing revenue/compliance today |
| **Buyer access** | Can you start conversations cheaply? | Behind 6-month enterprise RFP | Founders/Ops leads on LinkedIn or Slack |
| **Willingness to pay** | Do they spend money on this problem? | "We built it in-house" crowd | $5k–$50k/project norms |
| **Competition moat** | Can you speak credibly in 10 questions? | Everyone does it | Few can diagnose root cause quickly |
| **Repeatability** | Can you productize delivery? | Every project bespoke | Same playbook, different names |

**Examples that pass:** Shopify/WooCommerce performance rescue for 7-figure stores · AI support-deflection for regulated SaaS · Postgres index/VACUUM tuning for analytics-heavy apps · Stripe idempotent billing migrations · HIPAA-aware onboarding for clinics going paperless.
**Examples that fail:** "Mobile apps" (everyone), "AI chatbots" (nobody pays without a problem), "pretty much anything blockchain" (nobody pays at all in 2026).

## 2. The Three Niche Archetypes

1. **Problem-first niches** — "stores losing checkout conversions", "SaaS support drowning in tickets". You sell the cure to a pain they feel weekly. Best paid speed.
2. **Stack-first niches** — "Next.js/PostgreSQL performance", "Shopify theme → headless migration". You sell expertise in a stack the buyer already bought.
3. **Industry-first niches** — "AI automation for dental practices", "compliance dashboards for care homes". Highest moat, longest trust cycle, but premium rates and referral-dense once established.

Pick **one**. "Problem-first + stack-first" is the practical sweet spot for engineers leaving employment.

## 3. Validating Before Committing (One Weekends' Work)

1. **Read 30 posts/threads** from your candidate niche's buyers (Reddit r/shopify, niche Slacks, LinkedIn). Write down the 10 most-repeated pain phrases, verbatim.
2. **Interview 3 practitioners** — coffee/virtual, 20 min, no pitch. Ask "what broke this month?" and "what would you pay to never see it again?" Their exact words become your positioning copy.
3. **Desk research pricing** — search Upwork + proposals ("completed jobs") for your niche; note budget ranges and scope norms.
4. **Check density** — Google `"help me" OR "looking for" "Shopify performance"` — if you find 20+ live requests, the niche breathes.
5. **Door-open test** — send 5 outreach messages positioned around one pain. If 2+ reply, proceed. If zero, the pain or access hypothesis is wrong — pivot.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Choosing "what you know" rather than "what they pay for"** — you know Kubernetes; they pay to fix broken Shopify checkouts. Choose the intersection deliberately.
- **Too broad** — "AI consulting" fails the buyer-access filter; nobody knows what they're buying.
- **No ongoing pain** — one-shot niches ("migrate Access to Postgres") dry up; prefer recurring ("AI support deflection for SaaS").
- **Ignoring the buyer's vocabulary** — write their words, not your engineering jargon.

## 5. Exercise

Score three candidate niches on the five filters, do the one-weekend validation on the best, and draft your six-word positioning statement: "I help **[buyer]** who **[pain]** get **[outcome]** **[proof/timeframe]**". Example: "I help 7-figure Shopify stores fix checkout bugs that leak revenue — 48-hour turnaround, guaranteed."

**Acceptance criteria.** Your chosen niche scores ≥7/10 on the filters, you have 10 verbatim pain phrases from real buyers, and your positioning statement passes the "would a stranger know exactly what I do?" test.
