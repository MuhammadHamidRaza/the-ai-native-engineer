Freelancing fails at the identity level before it fails at the skill level. This subchapter separates the two questions every aspiring independent consultant must answer honestly: "Can I do this work?" (you already can — Chapters 00–08 exist to make sure of it) and "Can I run this business?" (risk, sales, delivery, get-paid — a different discipline with real parts).

## Lessons in Subchapter 39.1

1. **[39.1.1 Employee Mindset vs Independent Consultant Mindset](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/39-client-discovery-freelancing-and-client-acquisition/39-1-understanding-freelancing-and-independent-work/39-1-1-employee-mindset-vs-independent-consultant-mindset):** Risk ownership, billable-ceiling math, the identity shift, your personal viability model, and when to jump vs build a side pipeline first.
2. **[39.1.2 Selling Engineering Outcomes Over Hours](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/39-client-discovery-freelancing-and-client-acquisition/39-1-understanding-freelancing-and-independent-work/39-1-2-selling-engineering-outcomes-over-hours):** The outcome reframe, pricing models from hourly to value-based, honest anchoring, and the one-page Statement of Outcome that kills scope creep.

## How to Use This Subchapter

Do the math in 39.1.1 honestly — on paper, not in your head. Then rewrite your last project's pitch in 39.1.2's format. If both pass your own inspection, you're ready for Chapter 37's habits — the client conversations ahead reward exactly that discipline.
