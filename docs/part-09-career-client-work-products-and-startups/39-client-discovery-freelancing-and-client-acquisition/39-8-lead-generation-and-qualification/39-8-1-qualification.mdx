Not every company with pain is a buyer. Qualification saves you weeks of wasted calls with people who can't or won't pay. This lesson gives you a 5-minute qualification framework and the disqualifying questions nobody asks out loud.

## 1. The BANT-Style Engineering Qualifier

| Dimension | Question | Red flag |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Budget** | "What's the budget range for this?" | "We're figuring it out" at $0 |
| **Authority** | "Who else needs to weigh in?" | "We'll discuss with the board" (you never meet them) |
| **Need** | "What happens if this doesn't get fixed?" | "It'll be nice to have" |
| **Timeline** | "When does this need to work?" | "No rush" (translation: never) |
| **Fit** | "Is this in my wheelhouse?" | Out-of-stack asks |

Score 0–2 each; ≥7 = pursue; 4–6 = nurture; ≤3 = pass politely.

## 2. The Disqualifying Questions Nobody Asks

- "What's your timeline for a decision?" — no answer = no decision.
- "What would make you not hire someone for this?" — reveals hidden veto.
- "Have you tried this before? Why did it fail?" — reveals what they actually need.
- "Who pays the invoice?" — follow money or follow nothing.

## 3. The Qualification Call Script (15 min)

1. Context — restate what you heard.
2. Need depth — current workaround, cost of status quo.
3. Budget — anchor: "Typical projects in this space run $X–$Y; does that align?"
4. Timeline & decision — who signs, by when?
5. Next step — clear action with date.

## 4. Common Failure Modes

- **Qualifying on hope** — "they seem interested" without budget/authority.
- **Skipping disqualify** — wasting calls on tire-kickers.
- **Selling before qualifying** — pitching to someone who can't buy.

## 5. Exercise

Pick 5 outreach replies in your inbox. Run each through the qualifier. Categorize pursue/nurture/pass. Write the disqualifying question you'd ask each.

**Acceptance criteria.** Your qualifier covers budget, authority, need, timeline, fit — and you can articulate the exact disqualifier for each lead.
