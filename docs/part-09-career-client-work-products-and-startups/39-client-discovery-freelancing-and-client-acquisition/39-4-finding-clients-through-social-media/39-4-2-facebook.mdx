Engineers dismiss Facebook as memes and arguments. That dismissal is a gift: the buyers — salon owners, restaurant groups, HVAC contractors, dental practices, nonprofit directors — ignore LinkedIn but run their business lives in Facebook groups and Messenger. The problem isn't reach; it's noise, etiquette, and low deal sizes. Treat it as a local-services and SMB channel, not high-ticket consulting, and it prints steady $500–$10k projects.

## 1. What Facebook Is Good For (and Not Good For) in 2026

Good for: local services (websites, booking, e-commerce, automation), community presence, referrals from group admins, city- and industry-specific groups, Marketplace for productized offers, Page posts for visibility. Not good for: enterprise buyers, technical architecture consulting, or passive inbound. Organic reach for Pages is minimal — groups are the reach mechanism now, not Pages.

## 2. Who Hires There and at What Deal Size

| Buyer | Typical deal size | What they buy |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Local service businesses | $500–$5k project, $100–$500/mo retainer | Website, booking, reviews, chat automation |
| Restaurants / retail | $1k–$8k | Online ordering, Shopify setup |
| Nonprofits | $500–$5k (grant-funded) | Donation pages, volunteer CRM |
| Real estate agents | $1k–$6k | IDX sites, lead funnels |

Practitioner ranges. Facebook clients are price-sensitive but loyal and fast to refer — two or three happy local clients compound, because local owners all know each other.

## 3. Setup That Matters

- **Personal profile:** real name, real photo, your city, plain-English bio ("I build booking websites and automations for [city] shops"). Buyers check this before messaging.
- **Business Page:** create one — enables Services, reviews, location, a "Get quote" button into Messenger. Set hours, service area, reviews.
- **Groups:** join 5–10 relevant ones (city business groups, "[City] Restaurant Owners", trades groups). Read each group's rules first.
- **Marketplace:** viable for fixed-scope productized offers ("Shopify setup for local retailers — $1,500, 2 weeks"). Scope in the title filters tire-kickers.
- Professional dashboard (if eligible) gives basic reach analytics.

## 4. Organic Reach Mechanics

Group posts reach members when they generate comments quickly; Facebook surfaces "meaningful interaction" over reactions. Posts opening with a local question or scenario get picked up; bare self-promotion gets removed. Captioned vertical video (30–90s) gets broader distribution than static images. Links are throttled — describe the offer in the post; link in comments or Messenger CTA. Timing: early morning (7–9am) and early evening (6–9pm) beat work hours for SMB owners scrolling after closing.

## 5. Lead Workflow: Community-First, Not Cold DM

1. **Lurk 1–2 weeks** in chosen groups; answer real questions ("what's fair for a booking site?", "getting Google reviews?") with usable answers.
2. **Become known in one niche** (e.g., dental practices) until owners tag you: "Ask Hamid."
3. **Soft offers:** on "need a website" threads, reply with 2–3 useful points and one DM invite. No pitch-with-prices in thread unless it's a vendor day.
4. **Vendor/promo days:** post your fixed-scope offer in designated promos with a case-study image.
5. **Messenger close:** move to DM; restate their problem in 3 lines; propose one fixed-scope option and one retainer option.

Worked example (group reply → DM):

```text
Group comment: "For a 4-chair salon you mostly need online booking +
an autopilot Google review flow — the website matters less than people
think. Happy to DM you what that setup costs, no obligation."

DM: "Hey Maria — as discussed: booking site + review automation, fixed
$1,800, live in 2 weeks. Or $150/mo including hosting and edits. Want a
10-min call Tuesday?"
```

## 6. Format Specifics

- Text + one image beats text alone; before/after screenshots are your local portfolio.
- Short Reels of a real result ("this shop went 0 → 40 online bookings") — face on camera, no stock footage.
- Hashtags barely matter in groups; name the city/neighborhood in text for search.
- Marketplace listings: price up front, scope in bullets, real photos, one CTA.

## 7. Limits, Risks, Bans, Scams

Groups remove unapproved promotion — repeat violations = removal; some groups ban by phone/page. Messenger rate-limits new threads; ~50 identical DMs/day invites review or restriction. Scam patterns: check overpayment, gift cards, crypto "developer" jobs — never engage. Verify business identity (address, license, working phone) before work. Messenger marketing must honor opt-outs; keep a manual suppression list.

## 8. KPIs and Weekly Cadence

| KPI | Practitioner range | Target |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Group answers posted | 5–10 | daily 15 min |
| DMs from threads | 2–6 | 3–4/week |
| Calls booked | 1–3 | weekly |
| Fixed-scope offers sent | 2–4 | weekly |
| Closed deals | 1–2/month typical | track monthly |

Cadence: 15 min/day in groups, one promo-day post/week, one case-study Reel/week, Messenger follow-ups at day 2 and day 7.

## 9. When NOT to Bother

Skip Facebook if your niche is funded startups, enterprise, or global remote; if you need $10k+ deals to make the math work; or if you won't build a local presence. It is a volume-and-referral channel with a deliberately low per-deal ceiling — and it fails you if you try to use it like LinkedIn.

## 10. Exercise

1. Join 5 groups where ideal local clients congregate; read the rules; answer 3 questions each over 7 days, zero self-promotion.
2. Set up a Business Page: services, area, reviews button, Messenger CTA.
3. Write a productized offer; post it in one vendor/promo thread.
4. Move 2 interested owners into Messenger with your 3-line scope summary.

**Acceptance criteria.** At least 3 substantive answers published; Page complete with booking CTA; one promo post live; two DM threads started with a written fixed-scope offer; day-2/7 follow-up sent. Success in 30 days = one paid discovery call or a closed project; if zero engagement after 10 answers, switch groups or niche — not effort.
