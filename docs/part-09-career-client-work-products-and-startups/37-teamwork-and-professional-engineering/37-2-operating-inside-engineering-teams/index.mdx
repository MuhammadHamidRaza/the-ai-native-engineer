Schools teach the individual; companies run on the team. This subchapter covers the social systems that make engineering organizations function: the three-way contract with product and design, mentoring that actually develops people instead of extracting from them, and the three rituals that separate professionals from heroes — code review, blameless postmortems, and composed incident response.

## Lessons in Subchapter 37.2

1. **[37.2.1 Cross-Functional Collaboration with Product & Design](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/37-teamwork-and-professional-engineering/37-2-operating-inside-engineering-teams/37-2-1-cross-functional-collaboration-with-product-and-design):** The three-way contract, the read-before-build protocol, feedback without ego, the escalation ladder for disagreements, and the launch cadence that keeps all three disciplines honest.
2. **[37.2.2 Mentorship and Continuous Engineering Growth](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/37-teamwork-and-professional-engineering/37-2-operating-inside-engineering-teams/37-2-2-mentorship-and-continuous-engineering-growth):** The mentoring loop, 1:1s that work, delegation that doesn't backfire, your own growth after the novelty wears off, and how to talk about career progression honestly.
3. **[37.2.3 Code Review, Blameless Postmortems and Incident Collaboration](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/37-teamwork-and-professional-engineering/37-2-operating-inside-engineering-teams/37-2-3-code-review-blameless-postmortems-and-incident-collaboration):** The reviewer's checklist and etiquette, the postmortem template and the five blameless rules, and the 15-minute incident structure with IC/operator/comms roles.

## How to Use This Subchapter

Pick one ritual from each lesson and install it this month — e.g., review-before-build DoD on the next feature, a written postmortem for the next incident, and one 1:1 structure change if you mentor. Team culture changes through repeated small rituals, not manifestos.
