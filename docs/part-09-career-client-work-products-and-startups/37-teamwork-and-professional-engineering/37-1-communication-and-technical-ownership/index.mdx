Engineering careers stall more often on communication than on code. This section teaches the two highest-leverage documents you will write in your first five years: the RFC (the proposal that invites challenge before commitment) and the design doc (the record of what was decided and why) — plus the honest math of estimation: decomposition, three-point ranges, and how to reset expectations without destroying trust.

## Lessons in Subchapter 37.1

1. **[37.1.1 Async Writing, RFCs and Design Docs](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/37-teamwork-and-professional-engineering/37-1-communication-and-technical-ownership/37-1-1-async-writing-rfcs-and-design-docs):** The 1–2 page RFC that gets read, the design-doc sections that matter, the review loop that produces decisions instead of threads, and why rejected RFCs are records, not waste.
2. **[37.1.2 Estimating Work and Managing Expectations](/book/part-09-career-client-work-products-and-startups/37-teamwork-and-professional-engineering/37-1-communication-and-technical-ownership/37-1-2-estimating-work-and-managing-expectations):** Decomposition, reference-class anchoring, PERT three-point estimation, spikes to buy down uncertainty, and the 25%-of-estimate checkpoint that separates honest updates from deadline-day crises.

## How to Use This Subchapter

Do not read these as theory. Each lesson ends in an exercise you complete on **your real work** — a live RFC for a current decision, a live estimate for a current task. Bring both to your next 1:1.
