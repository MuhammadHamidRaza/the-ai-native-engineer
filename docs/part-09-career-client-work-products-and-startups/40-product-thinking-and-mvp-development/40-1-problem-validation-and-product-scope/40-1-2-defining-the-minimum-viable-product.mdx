An MVP is not a small product — it is the **smallest thing that tests your riskiest assumption**. This lesson scopes CareQueue (validated in 40.1.1) into a two-week MVP, and gives you the scoping tools to do the same for your own idea.

## 1. The MVP Formula

**MVP = Core value loop + Minimum surface to deliver it − Everything else.** A to-do list for your MVP is a to-do list for version 1, which means you are not building an MVP yet.

The core value loop for CareQueue, in one sentence:

> The front desk opens the inbox and sees today's messages pre-sorted into **Billing / Scheduling / Admin / Needs Human**, with a one-click draft reply for the billing ones.

Everything that is not that loop — patient records, insurance eligibility verification, multi-location management, billing analytics — is v2. If you cannot write your loop in one sentence with "and sees … with one …", you don't understand it yet.

## 2. The One-Job Rule

If your MVP does more than one job for one persona, it is not an MVP.

| Persona | Job | In MVP? |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Office manager (Dana) | Triage the `frontdesk@` inbox + one-click billing drafts | **Yes — the whole MVP** |
| Billing specialist | Read full thread history + attach EOB | v2 |
| Practice owner | Weekly savings report | v2 |
| Patients | Self-service booking | Never (not your product) |

## 3. Prioritization: RICE for the MVP Backlog

Score each candidate feature 0–10 on **Reach** (patients/messages affected), **Impact** (pain removed), **Confidence** (how sure you are), and divide by **Effort** (engineer-weeks). Keep the top 3; cut the rest.

| Feature (CareQueue) | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort | RICE |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| Inbox triage + labels | 10 | 10 | 9 | 1.5 | 60 |
| One-click billing draft | 6 | 9 | 8 | 1.5 | 29 |
| Morning digest email ("these 3 need you today") | 10 | 6 | 9 | 0.5 | 108 |
| Insurance claim auto-attach | 4 | 8 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| Multi-location rollup | 2 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| Patient self-service booking | 7 | 3 | 5 | 3 | 4 |

**MVP scope:** morning digest (RICE 108) + triage labels (60) + billing drafts (29). **Cut:** insurance auto-attach (unverified assumption — ask first), multi-location, self-service.

Exercise discipline: after RICE, run the "**cut until it hurts**" pass — remove the smallest RICE item; if you can survive without it, it wasn't in the MVP either.

## 4. The Slice vs the Whole

| Approach | Time | Learning |
| :-- | --: | :-- |
| Full dashboard rebuild (all 3 features polished) | 6 weeks | late and expensive |
| Thin slice: digest email only (no app) | 3 days | **highest per dollar** |
| Concierge MVP: you triage their inbox by hand for 2 weeks | 0 days build | the highest possible — real pain, real workflow |

**The concierge option deserves emphasis.** For CareQueue, the fastest possible validation is: Dana forwards you the inbox for two weeks (with patient identifiers stripped), and **you** sort it by hand plus draft replies in a shared doc. You learn the exact label taxonomy, the false-positive patterns, and the edge cases no spec will ever surface — for zero code written. Only when Dana says "this saved me an hour a day, when can I have it in Gmail?" do you automate the sorting.

## 5. The MVP Spec (One Page)

Every MVP gets a one-page spec before the first commit. Structure:

```text
PROBLEM: Office manager opens 60–80 messages/day; ~1 hour/day lost sorting billing questions.
RISKIEST ASSUMPTION: Sorting + drafting can be automated accurately enough that Dana trusts the output.
PERSONA: Dana, office manager, 3-chair dental practice, Gmail/Workspace.
CORE LOOP: Morning email digest → triage labels → one-click billing drafts → Dana approves/edits.
IN SCOPE: digest (07:30 daily), 4 labels, draft generation for billing, Dana's feedback loop ("wrong/right" one-click).
OUT OF SCOPE: insurance attach, multi-location, analytics, self-service booking — all v2.
SUCCESS: Dana reports "saves me ≥30 min/day" after 2 weeks, and ≥70% of drafts approved with edits <20%.
BUILD TIME: 2 weeks (digest 0.5w, triage 1w, drafts 0.5w).
```

## 6. Requirements Without Boiling the Ocean

Functional requirements for the MVP, written as testable statements:

- The system reads the last 7 days of messages in `frontdesk@` (Gmail API, read-only scope).
- Each message gets exactly one label: `Billing`, `Scheduling`, `Admin`, `Needs Human`.
- A billing-labeled message gets a draft reply in Dana's tone, ≤120 words, citing the billing knowledge base.
- The digest email lists today's `Needs Human` items first, then billing drafts, then the rest.
- Dana can mark a label wrong; wrong labels are logged and retrain nothing yet (v2).

Non-functional:

- Drafting latency < 10s per message; digest delivery by 07:30 ±5 min.
- No message bodies leave the practice's Workspace tenant (data residency per 36.2.1).
- Weekly cost ≤ $25/practice at current model pricing (36.1.2).

## 7. Common Failure Modes

- **MVP = miniature v1** — if it has dashboards, roles, and settings, it isn't minimum.
- **No riskiest assumption** — building breadth instead of testing the one thing that decides everything ("can AI triage dental billing accurately?").
- **Skipping the concierge step** — two weeks of manual sorting teaches more than any spec.
- **Success = "users like it"** — without a measurable target (≥30 min/day saved, ≥70% draft approval), you'll build forever.

## 8. Exercise

Write the one-page MVP spec for your own product (from 40.1.1's verdict):

1. One-sentence core loop.
2. RICE-score 6 candidate features; keep 3; justify the cuts.
3. Fill in the spec skeleton above completely — especially **riskiest assumption** and **success metric**.
4. Run the "cut until it hurts" pass once and record what you removed.
5. Write the concierge variant: how could you deliver this by hand for 2 weeks?

**Acceptance criteria.**
- [ ] Core loop fits one sentence and names one persona
- [ ] RICE table with 6 features, top 3 kept, cuts justified in one line each
- [ ] Spec names one riskiest assumption and one falsifiable success metric with a date
- [ ] Concierge variant described in ≤3 sentences
- [ ] Out-of-scope list contains at least 3 tempting-but-cut items
