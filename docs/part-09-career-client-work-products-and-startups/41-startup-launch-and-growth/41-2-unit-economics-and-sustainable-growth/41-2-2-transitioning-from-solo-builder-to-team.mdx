The hardest transition isn't product-market fit — it's people. This lesson teaches you when to hire your first human at CareQueue (or your product), what to hand over first, how to keep your unit economics alive while payroll starts eating margin, and how to delegate without losing your taste.

## 1. When to Hire (With Real Numbers)

**The trigger:** you've been the bottleneck for 3+ consecutive weeks, and the business can fund 6+ months of the salary at current profit (or committed pilots). Never hire because "it's the next step"; hire because a measurable constraint is costing you money or customers.

CareQueue worked example:

- Pilot cohort: 5 practices, ~20 hrs/week of founder time (setups, triage tuning, support).
- You personally hit a wall: onboarding backlog 2 practices deep; last pilot's weekly report is late; you haven't shipped in 2 weeks.
- Unit economics (41.2.1): $95/mo gross per logo × 25 logos × 20 months lifetime ≈ opportunity at ~$2,375 LTV per logo lost to slow onboarding.
- First hire options: (a) fractional customer-success/onboarding specialist, 15 hrs/week, $40/hr ≈ $600/mo — pays for itself if it saves ONE logo per quarter; (b) part-time contract engineer, $60/hr; (c) full-time when MRR ≥ $6k and 6+ months runway.

**Decision rule:** hire for the **value bottleneck**, not the ego bottleneck. If support emails are eating you, hire support help (hours, cheap). You are usually not the bottleneck for "writing code" — you're the bottleneck for "things that you do that only you currently can do", and that is onboarding, sales, and trust.

**Cash-first rule:** at $99/mo, one logo = $99/mo. A $1,500/mo fractional hire needs 15 saved/converted logos per year to break even at your margin — plan the hire to *create* that many.

## 2. Contractors vs Full-Time vs Fractional

| Model | When | Risk |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Fractional (part-time contract)** | First gap that is bounded and repetitive (onboarding, support triage, bookkeeping) | Scope creep; availability |
| **Contractor (project)** | Defined build with an end (design a landing page, build the eval harness) | Handoff debt if they vanish |
| **Full-time IC** | You have 6+ months runway AND recurring 40h/week of defined work | Salaries + payroll tax + management time |
| **Co-founder** | You want leverage, not just help — equity, not a salary | The hardest one to undo |

**Do not hire full-time before you need full-time.** A fractional specialist for onboarding is the safest first hire for an SMB SaaS like CareQueue.

## 3. The Delegation Ladder

1. **Do-this-task:** "Triage this inbox by 9am, here's the checklist." Verify everything.
2. **Own-this-outcome:** "Own onboarding for the next 2 pilot practices — first response within 4h, setup call scheduled in 48h, first digest by day 3." Verify at checkpoints (Friday 15-min sync).
3. **Own-this-function:** "You own customer success end-to-end — onboarding, weekly check-ins, churn-risk flags, renewal calls." Verify by metrics (activation ≥70%, week-4 NPS, churn < 4%/mo).
4. **Co-owner:** equity-aligned partner who owns revenue or product. Verify by the quarterly review, not by tasks.

**Every rung requires: written scope, named metric, checkpoint cadence, and an explicit "what never gets delegated" list (customer truth, pricing, vision).** If you can't write the rung down, you can't delegate it.

## 4. The 90-Day First-Hire Plan

**Week 1 — Watch and document.** The hire shadows you on every onboarding; you narrate your own decisions out loud ("I chose this label because…"). They write nothing; they ask questions; they read the runbook.

**Week 2 — One supervised loop.** They run one onboarding end-to-end with you on the call but silent. You only fix what breaks.

**Weeks 3–4 — One solo loop, daily check-ins.** They own one practice. You spot-check the digest output every morning before it sends.

**Weeks 5–8 — Ownership with metrics.** They own onboarding for new pilots. Your involvement: 15-min Friday sync + async Slack thread. Metrics: setup time (&lt;48h), day-3 activation (≥70%), first-30-day churn (&lt;10%).

**Weeks 9–12 — Prove it.** They run a full onboarding alone, start the churn-risk early-warning (no draft approvals for 5 days → flag), and write the v1.1 onboarding checklist themselves.

**Compensation for a fractional onboarding specialist (CareQueue example):** $40/hr × 15 hrs/week ≈ **$2,400/mo**, 3-month trial, renew monthly at your option, with a written scope and kill-switch ("either side can end with 2 weeks notice"). Alternative: $1,200/mo retainer + $150 per activated pilot practice — outcome-based, aligned with your funnel.

**Culture-in-3-bullets in the offer:** (1) Ship small, measure everything. (2) Customer truth beats founder conviction. (3) Say "I don't know" early, fix it fast.

## 5. Hiring Red Flags and Green Flags

| Red flag | Green flag |
| :-- | :-- |
| Needs to "own everything" from day 1 | Wants to shadow first, then own one practice |
| No questions about the product | Asks about *why* CareQueue wins against a VA |
| Negotiates salary before understanding the work | Asks about success metrics and failure modes |
| Never shipped customer-facing work | Has run a support inbox or onboarding flow before |
| Vague about availability | "I can do Tue/Thu/Fri mornings; I need the rest for my other client" |

## 6. Common Failure Modes

- **Hiring before revenue covers it** — payroll kills solo products; fund it from pilots first.
- **Delegating taste** — you can delegate setup and support; you cannot delegate "what this product is". Keep that.
- **No runbook before handing over** — if your onboarding lives in your head, the hire becomes a slower you.
- **Skipping week 1 shadowing** — the fastest way to pay someone to do it wrong.
- **No success metric in writing** — you'll disagree about whether they're working, exactly when it matters.

## 7. Exercise

Write the first-hire package for CareQueue (or your product):

1. The one sentence naming the bottleneck ("onboarding backlog is 2 practices deep and growing").
2. The JD for a fractional onboarding specialist: mission, 3 outcomes in 90 days, culture in 3 bullets, comp range with trial terms.
3. The Week 1 shadowing agenda for one specific practice.
4. The 90-day metrics table (setup time, day-3 activation, week-4 retention).
5. Your "never delegate" list — the three things that remain yours even at 20 team members.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- [ ] JD ≤1 page with mission, 3 measurable 90-day outcomes, comp range, trial terms
- [ ] Shadowing agenda names the practice and the two days
- [ ] Metrics table has 3 numeric thresholds and a review cadence
- [ ] "Never delegate" list has 3 items with one-line reasons each
- [ ] Offer can be sent as-is without legal review (use a standard contractor template)
