A great product with no distribution is a hobby. This lesson teaches you to position CareQueue (or your own product) so a stranger understands it in five seconds, pick the one channel where your buyers actually hang out, and run a 30-day launch that produces evidence — not just noise.

## 1. Positioning Is an Answer to "Why You, Why Now, Why Not the Alternative"

The formula everyone quotes is fine but useless without the work behind it. Positioning that survives contact with a real buyer answers three questions:

1. **For whom?** (One persona, narrow enough that the buyer says "that's me".)
2. **Instead of what?** (The alternative they use today — status quo, VA, built-in tool, Nothing.)
3. **Why is this better for them specifically?** (Differentiated value they care about, in their words.)

CareQueue worked example:

```text
ALTERNATIVE: VA / frontdesk hire / built-in auto-reply / manual triage
DIFFERENTIATED VALUE: cuts ~45 minutes/day of billing-question triage;
  understands dental billing codes; drafts replies in the practice's tone,
  approved in one click by the office manager.
```

**One-sentence positioning:**

> "For dental practice office managers who drown in billing emails, CareQueue triages the shared inbox and drafts the billing replies — so the practice answers 2x faster without hiring frontdesk help."

Notice: no tech stack, no features, no "AI-powered platform". The buyer's radio plays *their* pain and *their* outcome.

## 2. The Dunford-Style Positioning Exercise (45 Minutes, Alone)

April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* method, compressed for a solo founder:

1. **List your true alternatives** — including "do nothing" and "hire someone". CareQueue buyers compared you against: status quo (drown), a $600–$1,200/mo VA, Dentrix/Denticon's built-in mail routing, Front with AI add-ons, Gemini in Workspace.
2. **List your unique attributes** — e.g., dental-billing-aware triage, Gmail-native, one-click approval, per-practice tone learning, $99/mo, HIPAA-aware data boundary.
3. **Map attributes to buyer value** — attribute → "so what?" → value in their language:
   - Dental-billing-aware triage → understands EOB/EOB-adjacent language → fewer misroutes
   - One-click approval → answer in 15 seconds, not 5 minutes → Angry patients call less
   - $99/mo vs $800 VA → recover 90% of a VA's email value at 12% of the cost
   - Gmail-native → nothing to install or learn → setup call is 15 minutes
4. **Pick the frame** — category you are a better version of, not a new category. CareQueue frames as "the email triage tool your practice's front desk deserves", not "AI agent for dentistry" (new categories require education budget you don't have).
5. **Read it the five-second test:** show the one-liner to someone outside the industry; if they can't say what it does and why they'd pay, rewrite.

**Deliverable:** a one-page positioning statement with alternatives, 5 attributes mapped to value, the frame, the one-liner, and the five-second-test result.

## 3. Distribution Channels — Ranked by Leverage for This Product

| Channel | Cost | Time to signal | Fit for CareQueue |
| :-- | --: | --: | :-- |
| Direct outreach (your 10 interviewees → their peers) | $0 | 2–4 weeks | **Best — warm pipeline exists** |
| Dental office manager Facebook/LinkedIn groups | $0 | 4–8 weeks | Good — niche watering holes |
| Partnerships (practice management consultants, IT MSPs) | $0–rev share | 4–12 weeks | Strong — they own the trust |
| Content + SEO | $0 | 3–6 months | Long-term; not for launch |
| Paid ads | $ | days | No — pre-PMF is guessing with money |
| Product Hunt | $0 | 1 day | No — buyers aren't there |

**Selection criteria:** where are buyers congregating today (not where you wish they were)? How cheap is a qualified conversation? How fast is feedback? Pick ONE for 30 days and measure **qualified calls, not impressions**.

CareQueue's 30-day channel: direct outreach to the 5 practices from 40.1.1 + their peers in the same dental group's other locations. One KPI: **5 discovery calls booked**. Everything else is execution against that KPI.

## 4. The Launch Sequence (Do Not Skip Steps)

1. **Week 0 — Soft launch:** 3–5 friendly interviewee practices; instrumented (40.2.1); daily touchpoints; fix everything they hate.
2. **Week 1–2 — Pilot cohort:** 5 paying pilots ($149/mo); two reference calls per practice; one case study in progress.
3. **Week 3 — Reference factory:** convert one pilot into a publishable case study ("Dana went from 90-minute morning inbox to 20-minute review").
4. **Week 4 — Public launch:** email list (from 40.1.1 waitlist) + the case study + one post in two dental-manager groups + 5 personalized outbound emails/day to the next 50 practices.

Each gate has one metric that decides whether you proceed:

| Gate | Metric to pass | If you fail |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Soft launch | 3 practices using it daily | Product broken — fix, don't launch |
| Pilot | ≥3 of 5 renew | Value not yet proven — re-scope |
| Public launch | ≥5 calls booked in week 4 | Channel/message mismatch — change both |
| 30 days | ≥2 pilots converted to annual | Price/packaging issue |

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Everything, everywhere all at once** — a launch is a focused test, not a fireworks show.
- **No measurement** — you can't tell if it worked; every launch is a coin flip forever.
- **Big bang before beta feedback** — launching confident and wrong burns exactly the early trust you need.
- **Features as marketing** — "we added Slack integration" is not distribution; "Dana saves 45 minutes before her first coffee" is.
- **Comparison-shopping in public** — never trash competitors; position on your value, praise the alternative's role honestly.

## 6. Exercise

1. Complete the § 2 positioning exercise for CareQueue (or your own product) in writing.
2. Choose your ONE channel with a written two-sentence rationale.
3. Draft your 4-week launch calendar with the § 4 gates and metrics filled in with your own numbers.
4. Write your one-liner five ways; run the five-second test on 2–3 non-industry people; pick the winner.
5. Draft the week-4 outbound email (subject + body, ≤120 words) using 39.6.1's structure.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- [ ] One-page positioning with 3+ named alternatives and mapped attributes
- [ ] One channel, one KPI, 30-day calendar with gates
- [ ] One-liner passes the five-second test with strangers
- [ ] Soft-launch plan names 3 friendly practices and an instrumentation date
- [ ] Launch metrics are numbers, not adjectives
