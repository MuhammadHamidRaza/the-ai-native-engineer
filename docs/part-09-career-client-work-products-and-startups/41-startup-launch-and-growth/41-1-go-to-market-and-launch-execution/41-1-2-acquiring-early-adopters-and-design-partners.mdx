Your first 10 customers are not buyers — they are design partners: practices (or buyers) who get a product in exchange for feedback, workflow access, and the right to be featured in your story. Structured well, they become your reference customers and case studies before you spend a dollar on marketing. Structured badly, they become freeloaders who consume your roadmap and never convert.

## 1. Design Partner vs Customer

| | Design Partner | Pilot Customer | Standard Customer |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Price | $0–50% off | 20–50% off (or pilot fee) | Full price |
| Gives | Weekly feedback, workflow access, a reference quote | Case-study data, a reference call | Payment |
| Duration | 8–12 weeks | 1–3 months | Indefinite |
| Deliverable for you | Product shape, proof, a founding story | Conversion to annual contract | Revenue + retention |
| Your commitment | Deep involvement (calls, fixes, custom help) | Priority fixes | Standard support |

**The pilot is the bridge between partner and customer:** one cohort (3–5), one shared start date, one shared end date, one case-study outcome. Everybody knows what "graduating" means.

## 2. Finding Your First 5 Partners

For CareQueue, the best partners are exactly the five practices you interviewed in 40.1.1. Your network is warm demand — use it before any cold channel.

Recruitment script (email, ≤120 words):

```text
Subject: CareQueue pilot — 5 seats, free for 90 days

Hi Dana,

You told me the billing inbox eats an hour of your morning and your VA kept
missing the insurance detail. I've built CareQueue around exactly that.

I'm opening 5 pilot spots (free for 90 days, with a case-study agreement).
You'd get: my setup help, weekly 15-min calls, and influence over the roadmap —
I triage your inbox by hand with you for the first 2 weeks to learn your
practice's tone, then automate it.

If that sounds useful, I'll send a one-page pilot agreement and a calendar link.

[Name]
```

- **Warm first:** your interviewees → their peers → practices in the same DSO/network, same metro, same specialty conferences.
- **Cold fallback:** 10–15 cold emails/week (39.6.1) after the warm list is exhausted.
- **Offer framing:** never say "free beta"; say "founding pilot — one of five practices shaping the product, in exchange for feedback." Scarcity and status, not charity.

**Pricing ladder for pilots:** 2 partners free (for case studies), 2 at $149/mo (to prove willingness to pay), 1 at full $99–149/mo (anchor for GA). Never 5 free; you'll never know if anyone pays.

## 3. The One-Page Design Partner Agreement

Keep it to one page. Every clause earns its place:

```text
PARTIES: [you] and Dana's practice ("the Practice").
TERM: 90 days from onboarding call. Renewable by mutual agreement.
FEE: $0 / $149/mo (circle one). Payment terms: net-14.
YOU GET: (1) weekly 20-min feedback call; (2) workflow access via read-only Gmail;
         (3) permission to feature the practice in anonymized case study materials.
THEY GET: (1) full CareQueue access; (2) your direct line for issues (same-day
         response in business hours); (3) locked-in founding price for 24 months.
SUCCESS CRITERIA: ≥30% of billing questions auto-drafted and approved by day 45;
                 Dana reports ≥30 min/day saved by day 60; 4+ feedback calls completed.
IP: You own CareQueue; the Practice owns its data. The Practice gets a perpetual,
    non-exclusive license to use while a customer. No patient data in any demo.
CASUALTY: either side may exit with 7 days' notice in writing; no penalties.
```

**IP note:** you own the product; the practice owns its data. Never sign anything assigning your product or your models to a customer, and never accept anything assigning their patient data to you. (If a contract drafts itself into something exotic, stop and get counsel — 36.2's rules apply in full.)

## 4. The Partner Rhythm (90 Days)

| Week | Your move | Their move |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 0–2 | Daily white-glove triage by hand; learn the practice's voice; fix obvious bugs | Forward inbox; flag wrong drafts; name edge cases |
| 3–4 | Automate labels; draft replies appear; weekly calls begin | Approve/reject drafts with feedback; mark wrong labels |
| 5–8 | First metric report; case-study interview; ask for 2 intros | 20-min Friday call; intro office managers in 2 peer practices |
| 9–12 | Graduation call: renew at founding price or convert to case study; upsell annual | Sign annual prepay; record 4-minute video testimonial |

**Graduation trigger:** the day-60 metric is hit and they answer "would you pay $149/mo? what would make that an easy yes?" with a real number. If the answer is a shrug, they're a reference customer, not a buyer — treat them as marketing material, keep them happy, but don't hold the roadmap for them.

## 5. Common Failure Modes

- **Partners who stop giving feedback** — the deal was feedback; if a partner ghosts, send one honest check-in ("Still want this, or should we close the pilot?") and move on if they don't answer.
- **More partners than you can white-glove** — five is the cap; each one costs you 15+ hours.
- **Free partners who never intended to pay** — that's why 2 of 5 are paid; paying is the only proof of seriousness.
- **No case-study path** — partners should know on day 1 that a case study is the goal; surprise asks kill trust.
- **Skipping the graduation call** — pipeline rots silently; calendar it before the pilot begins.

## 6. Exercise

1. List your 5 candidate design partners with one sentence each on why they feel this pain today.
2. Send the recruitment email to your first 3 (personalized per practice — no blast).
3. Draft the one-page partner agreement above for "your" product.
4. Build the 90-day partner rhythm calendar with your own dates.
5. Write the graduation call script (≤10 lines) for the day-60 metric hit.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- [ ] 5 named partners with pain-specific reasons
- [ ] 3 personalized emails sent (copy enclosed)
- [ ] Agreement covers scope, cadence, IP, fee, success criteria — one page
- [ ] 90-day calendar with week-by-week actions
- [ ] Graduation script names the day-60 metric and a direct ask for the annual commitment
